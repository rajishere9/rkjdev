Google Rolls Out Universal Cart Across Search and Gemini in US

The intelligent shopping hub links products across multiple retailers, tracks price drops, and coordinates checkout through the Universal Commerce Protocol.

In short

  • Universal Cart allows United States shoppers to collect items across different retailers within Google Search and the Gemini app into a single unified cart.
  • The feature runs on Google's Shopping Graph of more than 60 billion listings to track price drops and inventory restocks automatically.
  • Google Wallet integration helps users evaluate credit card reward perks and surfaces available promotional codes.
  • Purchases can be completed directly through Google Pay via the Universal Commerce Protocol or transferred to merchant sites, with retailers remaining the seller of record.

On October 15, 2026, Google officially launched its Universal Cart feature for users in the United States, integrating a cross-retailer shopping hub directly into Google Search and the Gemini app. The rollout follows an initial preview at the Google I/O conference in May 2026. Designed to streamline how users browse and purchase goods across the web, the tool allows shoppers to gather products from separate online stores into a single digital cart instead of juggling multiple open tabs and mobile screenshots.

According to an announcement by Suresh Ganapathy, Google's Senior Director of Product Management for Consumer Shopping, Google is dividing its commerce efforts into two tracks: building agentic features that take action on behalf of consumers, and delivering practical utilities that give shoppers direct control. Universal Cart focuses on the latter, providing a persistent repository that preserves shopping sessions across multiple Google entry points.

How the Universal Cart Works Across Search and Gemini

The Universal Cart acts as an intelligent aggregator across various Google properties. When a user finds an item through AI Mode in Google Search or during a conversational session inside the Gemini app, they can save it directly to the shared cart. For example, Google noted that a user might save a Steve Madden handbag from Search AI Mode, and later add an Ulta Beauty lip gloss or a Quince sweater inside Gemini. Both items will stay stored together in the unified cart when the user returns later.

As reported by 9to5Google, the feature introduces noticeable interface additions across Google's ecosystem. A shopping cart icon now appears in the top-right corner of the google.com homepage and desktop search result pages, as well as on desktop Chrome's New Tab Page. Mobile users can access the cart to the left of the profile switcher in the Google app for Android, while Gemini users will find a dedicated "Cart and orders" option inside the account menu.

Tracking Deals and Evaluating Payment Perks

Beyond storing product links, the Universal Cart proactively works in the background by connecting directly to the Google Shopping Graph, which maintains more than 60 billion constantly refreshed product listings. The cart uses this index to monitor real-time availability and notify consumers if a saved item drops in price or returns to inventory after selling out.

The cart also incorporates financial intelligence powered by Google Wallet. For shoppers who carry multiple credit cards, the system analyzes stored payment methods to highlight which card provides the highest reward points, cash back, or specific perks for the selected transaction. Additionally, the interface surfaces relevant promotional codes directly inside the cart before the user finalizes the order, as detailed by TechCrunch.

Checkout via Universal Commerce Protocol

When consumers decide to finalize their orders, the system provides two distinct paths. Shoppers can complete a quick checkout directly inside the cart using Google Pay, or they can choose to transfer the items directly into the merchant's native checkout flow on the retailer's website. Supported retailers offering simplified checkout include Target, Walmart, Nike, Sephora, Wayfair, Ulta Beauty, and numerous merchants hosted on Shopify.

Google emphasized that regardless of the checkout path chosen, the external merchant remains the official seller of record. Customers receive direct order confirmations and post-purchase customer service directly from the participating retailer rather than Google, as reported by CNET. The checkout process is governed by the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), an open standard Google introduced earlier in 2026 designed to help AI agents and retailers work together across different parts of the shopping journey, including checkout.

Broader Rollout Plans and Industry Context

Universal Cart arrives just ahead of Black Friday and the peak holiday shopping season, a window when major technology platforms routinely attempt to capture consumer spending. Rival firms, including OpenAI and Meta, have introduced their own agentic shopping assistants designed to assist or execute purchases online, while Shopify has similarly broadened its platform to allow third-party AI agents to browse listings, according to The Verge.

While the current release is restricted to users in the United States using Search and Gemini, Google previously outlined plans to expand the cart experience into YouTube and Gmail. According to details shared by Unite.AI from the initial Google I/O presentation by Vidhya Srinivasan, Vice President and General Manager of Ads and Commerce, Google also intends to extend UCP-backed checkout to international markets, starting with Canada and Australia, followed by the United Kingdom, alongside future expansions into local food delivery and hotel reservations.

Questions people ask

Where can users access the Universal Cart?

Universal Cart is currently available in the United States across Google Search results, the google.com homepage, desktop Chrome's New Tab Page, the Google Android app, and the Gemini mobile application menu.

Does Google become the retailer when you purchase through Universal Cart?

No. Participating retailers like Target, Walmart, Nike, and Sephora remain the seller of record, and customers receive order fulfillment, receipts, and support directly from those merchants.

How does Universal Cart find discounts?

The cart tracks product listings through Google's Shopping Graph to alert users to price drops, discovers relevant promo codes, and uses Google Wallet to recommend which credit card earns the best perks or cash back.
