Intel to Acquire Quantum Software Firm Classiq in $2.2B Deal

The deal marks the largest software acquisition in quantum computing to date and sets up Tel Aviv as Intel's global quantum development hub.

In short

  • Intel entered into a definitive agreement on October 26, 2026, to acquire Israeli quantum software firm Classiq.
  • Financial reports value the cash-and-stock transaction at between $2.2 billion and $2.3 billion, though Intel did not disclose terms in its official announcement.
  • Classiq's automated algorithm synthesis platform will merge with Intel's silicon spin-qubit hardware to create an end-to-end quantum computing stack.
  • The acquisition is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory reviews, after which Classiq will become Intel's global quantum development center.

Intel Corporation announced on October 26, 2026, that it has reached a definitive agreement to acquire Tel Aviv-based quantum software startup Classiq Technologies Ltd. The transaction aims to unite Classiq's hardware-agnostic algorithm synthesis platform with Intel's silicon spin-qubit hardware, creating a full-stack quantum computing business, according to an official press release from Intel.

While Intel's announcement did not disclose financial terms, reports from SiliconANGLE and Globes peg the transaction value at $2.2 billion. Israeli news outlet Calcalist via ynetglobal reported the total valuation at $2.3 billion in cash and shares, while Globes reported that approximately $100 million in cash on hand effectively reduces the $2.3 billion purchase price to $2.2 billion. The transaction is the largest acquisition in the history of the quantum computing sector, surpassing IonQ's roughly $1 billion acquisition of Oxford Ionics in 2025.

Unifying Silicon Spin-Qubits with Algorithm Automation

Classiq, founded in 2020 by chief executive Nir Minerbi, chief product officer Amir Naveh, and chief technology officer Dr. Yehuda Naveh, develops high-level software that automates the generation of quantum circuits. Classiq provides high-level modeling and design automation that allows developers to create scalable quantum applications without deep expertise in quantum circuit design. According to The Quantum Insider, Intel likened this approach to the advent of Electronic Design Automation (EDA) and Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tools in classical semiconductor engineering.

Intel has developed quantum computing technology internally since 2015. Unlike competitors focusing on superconducting circuits or trapped ions, Intel manufactures silicon spin qubits and cryogenic control electronics using standard commercial semiconductor fabrication processes. According to Intel's announcement, adding Classiq's synthesis platform enables the company to offer Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) alongside its existing portfolios of central processors (CPUs), graphics chips (GPUs), and custom ASICs.

Following the close of the transaction, Classiq will become Intel's global development center for quantum computing, as reported by Globes. Intel stated that it plans to preserve Classiq's hardware-agnostic stance, continuing to support third-party hardware modalities such as trapped ions, photons, and superconducting processors.

Commercial Deployments and Existing Customers

Classiq had raised roughly $200 million prior to the buyout and was valued between $600 million and $700 million in its 2025 financing round, according to ynetglobal. Major shareholders include venture firms Entrée Capital, which holds a 15% stake, Team8 with an 8% stake, and Israeli institutional investor Phoenix Group with 7%.

Intel chief executive Lip-Bu Tan was an early personal investor in Classiq, participating in its $33 million Series B round in February 2022 and its $110 million Series C round in May 2025, according to reporting by SiliconANGLE. Sources cited by Calcalist indicated that Tan recused himself from acquisition negotiations due to his preexisting financial holdings.

Although its annual revenue remains below $100 million according to Calcalist via ynetglobal, Classiq has secured commercial and research deployments across several sectors, according to Globes:

  • Automotive: BMW utilized Classiq's software to simulate aerodynamic performance and material variations, reducing reliance on real-world physical testing.
  • Financial Services: HSBC built quantum algorithms designed for transaction fraud detection, while Italian lender Intesa Sanpaolo deployed the software to model options pricing over tighter timeframes.
  • Corporate and Academia: Classiq counts Citibank, Deloitte, and Rolls-Royce among its commercial partners, alongside more than 600 academic institutions, including research groups at MIT and University College London.

Competitive Field and Closing Timeline

By acquiring Classiq, Intel establishes direct competition with enterprise quantum software stacks developed by hardware rivals. IBM maintains its Qiskit platform, which also supports hardware-agnostic development workflows, while rival Horizon Quantum Computing has raised $22 million, as reported by Globes. Meanwhile, Nvidia has focused on its CUDA Quantum environment, which acts as a bridge between classical graphics processors and external quantum systems rather than proprietary quantum hardware.

Intel and Classiq will operate as separate organizations until regulatory clearances and customary closing terms are finalized. According to Morningstar's report on the Dow Jones dispatch, the companies anticipate closing the transaction in the first half of 2027.

Questions people ask

How much is Intel paying to acquire Classiq?

Intel did not disclose financial terms in its official announcement. However, reports from SiliconANGLE and Globes value the transaction at $2.2 billion, while Calcalist reported a $2.3 billion deal value in cash and shares, and Globes reported that Classiq's cash on hand effectively reduces the $2.3 billion price to $2.2 billion.

Will Classiq software only run on Intel quantum hardware after the deal closes?

No. Intel stated in its announcement that it intends to continue supporting Classiq's existing multi-platform integrations and maintain a hardware- and modality-agnostic software architecture.

When is the acquisition expected to close?

The transaction is subject to regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions, with completion expected in the first half of 2027.
