US Links Megaspeed Exec to Beijing Espionage Targeting Jensen Huang

Intelligence agencies tracked Megaspeed executive Alice Huang's ties to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, though the White House chose not to intervene.

In short

  • U.S. intelligence agencies determined that Megaspeed executive Alice Huang acted as a Chinese intelligence asset and developed a close relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.
  • Megaspeed was already under federal investigation for allegedly helping smuggle $2 billion worth of advanced Nvidia AI chips to China.
  • President Donald Trump was briefed on the intelligence findings a year ago but opted against taking action to avoid hurting Nvidia sales or antagonizing Beijing.
  • Nvidia stated that interactions with Alice Huang involved standard customer relations and that no sensitive trade secrets or national security information were shared.

U.S. intelligence agencies believe that a top executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed acted as a Chinese intelligence asset while cultivating a close relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. According to an investigation by The New York Times reported by The Guardian, American officials monitored Alice Huang—also known as Huang Le—during an inquiry into the illicit diversion of advanced artificial intelligence hardware to China. Officials concluded that she gathered commercial intelligence for Beijing, raising alarms inside the White House because of Jensen Huang's prominent role in U.S. technology leadership and his direct relationship with President Donald Trump.

Surveillance and Smuggling Allegations Around Megaspeed

The inquiry into Alice Huang expanded out of an export control investigation into Megaspeed, a Singaporean artificial intelligence infrastructure company that operates as an Nvidia cloud partner. Megaspeed builds data centers and leases computing power to Chinese corporations, including Alibaba. As reported by Tom's Hardware, the firm came under federal scrutiny after being accused of helping smuggle $2 billion worth of restricted Nvidia processors into China last year.

The U.S. Department of Commerce initiated its review after officials inspected a Megaspeed data center facility in Malaysia. Inspectors discovered ordered Nvidia processors still packaged in original shipping crates, leading investigators to suspect that the components were intended for immediate re-export rather than local deployment. Reporting from Engadget noted that Megaspeed's Malaysian sister affiliate purchased at least $4.8 billion in Nvidia-based hardware in less than two years, a volume The New York Times described as unusual for an obscure firm.

During electronic surveillance of the executive, counterintelligence personnel observed multiple contacts between Alice Huang and Jensen Huang. The two individuals share a surname but are unrelated. Photographic records cited by Tom's Hardware show Alice Huang seated beside Jensen Huang at an informal event around Computex 2024, as well as leaving a Taipei restaurant together with business associates around Computex 2025.

The White House Response and Counterintelligence Decisions

U.S. intelligence agencies briefed President Donald Trump on Alice Huang's alleged espionage role a year ago. According to reporting from Engadget, Trump decided not to take action. Officials familiar with the matter stated that the administration was wary of disrupting Nvidia's commercial sales or aggravating trade friction with Beijing.

American officials do not believe Jensen Huang had any awareness of Alice Huang's alleged intelligence connections. Despite this assessment, the government chose not to provide the Nvidia CEO with a defensive briefing—a standard advisory warning given to American citizens targeted by hostile foreign intelligence operations. Officials withheld the briefing because doing so might have alerted Alice Huang and compromised active surveillance efforts, according to CNET.

The Chinese embassy in Washington dismissed the reports, describing the espionage accusations as groundless smears and slanders against China.

Nvidia's Position and Industry Fallout

Nvidia responded to the revelations by defending its executive engagements as routine corporate protocol. In statements provided to CNET and Tom's Hardware, an Nvidia spokesperson stated that customer meetings fall under normal executive duties.

"Meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job; Jensen talks with thousands of customer executives every year," the company stated. "Megaspeed has been no different than many other regional cloud customers. Our regional cloud customers receive ordinary business information—not sensitive Nvidia trade secrets—and zero information relevant to national security."

Nvidia added that it regularly receives intelligence alerts from federal regulators and complies with government directives: "We're confident that if the U.S. government believes that any person is a threat to American industry, it would let us know."

The case underscores intensifying friction surrounding artificial intelligence hardware and international security controls. While the Biden administration banned sales of advanced AI processors to China in 2022, the Trump administration eased some export rules in 2025, taking a 15% revenue share from certain approved chip sales by Nvidia and AMD. Despite these adjustments, illicit supply networks continue operating, placing key commercial leaders directly in the path of foreign intelligence operations.

Questions people ask

Who is Alice Huang and what is her relation to Jensen Huang?

Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, is an executive and managing director at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company. She has no family relationship to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.

Did Jensen Huang knowingly assist Chinese intelligence?

No. U.S. officials do not believe Jensen Huang was aware of Alice Huang's alleged intelligence activities, and Nvidia stated that only ordinary commercial information was discussed during standard customer interactions.

Why didn't the U.S. government warn Jensen Huang about the investigation?

Federal counterintelligence agencies decided against providing a defensive briefing to Jensen Huang to prevent alerting Alice Huang, as active surveillance and investigations into her actions were still underway.

What was Megaspeed accused of doing before the espionage claims?

Megaspeed was under U.S. Department of Commerce investigation for allegedly assisting in smuggling $2 billion worth of restricted Nvidia AI chips to China, following an inspection that found boxed hardware sitting in a Malaysian data center.
