Super Micro Export Case Defendant Pleads Guilty Over AI Shipments

Ting-Wei 'Willy' Sun admitted to four federal counts tied to an illicit operation that used dummy servers to route restricted Nvidia processors to China.

In short

  • Ting-Wei 'Willy' Sun pleaded guilty to four federal counts in Manhattan for his role in diverting $2.5 billion in restricted AI hardware to China.
  • The operation involved swapping serial numbers with hair dryers onto dummy server chassis to deceive export inspectors.
  • Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan 'Wally' Liaw pleaded not guilty, while Super Micro itself has not been charged.
  • Sentencing for Sun is scheduled for September 8, 2027, following a plea agreement with federal prosecutors.

A key defendant in the federal Super Micro export case has admitted guilt for his role in a multi-billion-dollar scheme to funnel restricted artificial intelligence technology to China. Ting-Wei "Willy" Sun, a third-party broker and former contractor, entered a guilty plea in a Manhattan federal court on October 8, 2026, according to court records reported by The Next Web.

The plea resolves criminal charges against Sun stemming from a March 2026 indictment that accused three individuals of conspiring to circumvent United States export controls. Federal prosecutors identified Sun as a central "fixer" in an operation that unlawfully diverted roughly $2.5 billion worth of advanced computing hardware, including servers equipped with restricted Nvidia processors, into Chinese territory.

Guilty Plea Details in the Super Micro Export Case

Appearing in Manhattan federal court, Sun pleaded guilty to four criminal counts: conspiring to violate U.S. export controls, smuggling goods, defrauding the United States, and obstructing justice, as reported by NDTV Profit. Sun entered the plea under an agreement with federal prosecutors, though specific cooperation details remain undisclosed.

According to Tom's Hardware, Sun's formal sentencing is scheduled for September 8, 2027. His defense counsel declined to comment to Bloomberg following the hearing.

Sun was indicted alongside two other individuals connected to server manufacturer Super Micro Computer Inc.: co-founder Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw and Ruei-Tsang "Steven" Chang, a former sales manager in the company's Taiwan office. Liaw pleaded not guilty and has since resigned from Super Micro's board of directors, while Chang has not appeared in court, according to The Next Web.

Dummy Servers and Serial Number Swaps

Federal prosecutors stated that the smuggling scheme originated around October 2023 or 2024 to evade strict export licensing rules imposed by the United States on high-performance accelerators. The indictment specifically named enterprise servers housing Nvidia B200, H100, and H200 graphics processing units, which are prohibited from unauthorized export to China due to national security regulations, according to reporting by Benzinga.

Court filings detailed an intricate deception strategy designed to bypass regulatory audits. The defendants routed servers through an intermediary entity in Southeast Asia, using a Thailand-based corporation to receive shipments, as detailed by Tom's Hardware.

To satisfy regulatory inquiries, the conspirators created dummy server enclosures. Workers used handheld hair dryers to peel serial number labels off genuine server chassis and transferred them to empty or mock units. In December 2025, Sun staged these dummy units to deceive an inspector from the U.S. Department of Commerce and made false statements during an official review, concealing the reality that the genuine hardware had already been rerouted to mainland China, according to NDTV Profit. Investigative filings reported that shipments were delivered to Chinese tech firm Alibaba, though Alibaba has denied receiving the banned hardware.

Corporate Position and Federal Enforcement

Super Micro Computer Inc. has not been named as a defendant in the criminal proceedings. In August 2026, the company stated that its current senior executive leadership was unaware of the diversion scheme and affirmed that it is cooperating with federal investigators, as noted by The Next Web.

The server maker confirmed earlier in 2026 that it had terminated Sun's contract and severed professional ties with the other indicted individuals, emphasizing that the legal action has not interrupted ongoing business operations, as reported by Benzinga.

The guilty plea occurs amid a wider campaign by federal authorities to shut down illicit semiconductor supply networks. Just one week prior to Sun's plea, federal prosecutors charged a California resident in an unrelated case involving the illegal transfer of $300 million in AI servers to China, according to The Next Web. With Sun now awaiting sentencing, attention turns to the pending charges against Liaw as the Department of Justice continues to prosecute illicit semiconductor diversions.

Questions people ask

What charges did Ting-Wei 'Willy' Sun plead guilty to?

Sun pleaded guilty to four federal counts: conspiracy to violate U.S. export controls, smuggling goods, defrauding the U.S. government, and obstructing justice.

What hardware was diverted in the smuggling scheme?

The indictment specified Super Micro servers equipped with restricted Nvidia AI processors, specifically B200, H100, and H200 GPUs.

Is Super Micro Computer charged in the case?

No, Super Micro is not named as a defendant. The company stated that its senior management had no knowledge of the scheme, terminated Sun, severed ties with the co-defendants, and is cooperating with authorities.

When is the sentencing set for Ting-Wei Sun?

Sun is scheduled to be sentenced in Manhattan federal court on September 8, 2027.
