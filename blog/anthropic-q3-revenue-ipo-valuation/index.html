The Claude maker told investors its quarterly sales reached $16.8 billion with adjusted profitability as it prepares for a potential $2 trillion public offering.

In short

  • Anthropic reported $16.8 billion in revenue for the third quarter of 2026, marking an increase of roughly 45% over the second quarter.
  • The company stated it achieved profitability on an adjusted basis as it prepares for an initial public offering expected in November.
  • Targeting a valuation that could approach $2 trillion, Anthropic would rank as the largest IPO in history if completed at that level.
  • Analysts caution that sequential quarter-over-quarter growth is tapering.

Anthropic informed prospective investors that it generated $16.8 billion in revenue during the third quarter of 2026, according to reporting from Business Insider. The maker of the Claude model family also disclosed that it reached profitability on an adjusted basis during the quarter. The financial update was presented during an investor meeting designed to build demand for an initial public offering scheduled for November 2026, which could seek a valuation approaching $2 trillion.

Anthropic's latest results represent a key health check for the generative artificial intelligence sector, where the early AI market has boosted stocks by trillions of dollars in value. While the Financial Times first reported the adjusted profitability and revenue figures, Anthropic declined to comment publicly on the numbers. Crucially, the investor presentation did not detail whether the $16.8 billion figure counts gross or net sales, nor did the company clarify the exact accounting methodology used to define adjusted profitability.

Anthropic Q3 Revenue Growth and Financial Trajectory

The third-quarter top line continues a steep expansion curve for Anthropic throughout 2026. Prior reporting from The Wall Street Journal indicated the company generated $4.8 billion in revenue in the first quarter of the year. Sales then more than doubled in the second quarter to approximately $11.6 billion. The $16.8 billion Q3 performance marks a sequential increase of around 45%.

Despite the scale of the expansion, market analysts point out that growth rates are moderating compared to earlier quarters. PitchBook private company analyst Harrison Rolfes told Business Insider that Anthropic is approaching an operational turning point. While the growth remains substantial, Rolfes noted that decelerating quarter-over-quarter gains could trim the valuation multiple Anthropic seeks during its public offering, particularly because it remains unclear how much capital the company spends on infrastructure to support those sales.

Anthropic has historically counted gross customer billings through cloud distributor partners toward its revenue metrics, even though the company does not pocket the entire sum.

Catalysts Driving Enterprise Adoption

Anthropic's rapid ascent has been driven by heavy enterprise adoption across its product line. Strong customer pickup for its developer-focused coding tool, Claude Code, alongside frequent model releases, helped propel the company into one of the fastest-scaling businesses on record. In May 2026, the company completed a $65 billion private funding round that set its private valuation at $965 billion.

Securing liquid capital remains essential for Anthropic to finance the high-performance accelerator chips and data center capacity needed to train and deploy advanced foundation models. Yet safety concerns grew following a researcher's resignation.

In early September 2026, Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned from the startup, issuing a public warning that builders in the sector genuinely fear extreme systemic risks from advanced systems by the end of the decade. Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei responded by publishing an essay urging the industry to moderate the speed of capability advancements so safety mechanisms and governance can mature alongside model power.

Competitive Pressure and Market Outlook

As Anthropic heads into its November listing window, the broader competitive environment is intensifying. Rival foundation model developers have shifted commercial dynamics across the sector. OpenAI has engaged in direct pricing competition with Anthropic, while Meta, Google, and SpaceXAI continue to ship frontier model iterations and specialized tools.

At the same time, improvements in open-source and open-weight architectures during 2026 have led some enterprise software teams to migrate workloads away from proprietary APIs to reduce operating costs.
