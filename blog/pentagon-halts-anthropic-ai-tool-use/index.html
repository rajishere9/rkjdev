Pentagon Halts Use of Anthropic AI Tools Across Military Systems

The US Department of Defense confirmed it has ceased using Anthropic models like Claude after blacklisting the startup over AI safety guardrail disputes.

Key takeaways

  • The Pentagon officially confirmed that it has ceased all use of Anthropic products across military operations and intelligence workflows.
  • The phase-out followed a February 2026 national security supply chain risk designation issued by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth when Anthropic refused to strip safety guardrails.
  • Anthropic models, including Claude and Mythos, had remained active inside Palantir's Maven Smart System until recently due to complex technical integrations.
  • The Department of Defense has signed alternative contracts with OpenAI, Google, and xAI, with OpenAI seeing broader adoption across military branches.

The US Department of Defense has officially stopped using Anthropic AI products across its operations, ending months of continued use following a government blacklisting of the artificial intelligence firm.

A Pentagon official confirmed the move in a statement on October 5, 2026, telling the BBC that the department "has ceased the use of Anthropic products." The decision concludes a prolonged phase-out that began earlier in the year when Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated the company a national security supply chain risk.

The Dispute Over AI Safety Guardrails

Anthropic had worked with the US government and military since 2024, becoming the first advanced AI company to have its models deployed in government agencies handling classified work. However, tensions escalated earlier in 2026 when the Pentagon pressured the company to eliminate built-in safety guardrails on its Claude models to grant defense personnel unrestricted access, according to reporting by The Times of India.

Anthropic refused the Pentagon's demands, citing internal policies against autonomous weaponry and mass surveillance. Following that refusal, Hegseth designated Anthropic as a "national security supply-chain risk" on February 27, 2026—a legal classification typically reserved for foreign adversaries. The Pentagon established a six-month deadline to phase out the company's software by late August.

Anthropic called the designation "unprecedented and unlawful" and filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration to overturn the measure. A spokesperson for Anthropic declined to comment on the Pentagon's October statement confirming the full cessation of tool use.

Why Military Claude Use Continued Past Deadlines

Despite the late-August phase-out timeline, defense personnel and contractors continued using Anthropic's Claude models—including its powerful Mythos models—as recently as the week prior to the official announcement, as reported by operativmm.az. Defense officials relied on Claude for research, intelligence gathering, and active military operations against Iran that began on February 28, 2026.

The delay in fully decoupling from Anthropic was driven largely by deep software integration rather than policy hesitations. Defense analysts utilized Claude inside the Maven Smart System, an intelligence and data platform operated by Palantir that synthesizes drone footage and satellite imagery. Military personnel used Claude on Maven to organize visual intelligence, identify potential military targets, and generate concise one-page operational briefs for senior commanders.

"The fact that it took the Pentagon until now to remove all use of Claude from across the system is indicative that these things are not just plug and play," Lauren Kahn, a senior research analyst at Georgetown University's Center for Security and Emerging Technology and former US defense official, told the BBC. "Once they become integrated it can be painful to remove them."

Shifting Military Contracts to OpenAI, Google, and xAI

As the Department of Defense worked to detach Claude from Palantir's Maven system, the Pentagon shifted procurement to alternative artificial intelligence providers. The military has signed contracts with Google, xAI, and OpenAI, according to Android Headlines.

People familiar with the defense department's AI usage noted that OpenAI tools have been adopted more widely across some military departments in recent months.

High-Level Talks at the White House

Even as the Pentagon severed operational ties and defended its supply chain designation in court, relations between Anthropic leadership and the executive branch showed signs of engagement. President Donald Trump met with Anthropic Chief Executive Dario Amodei twice at the White House during the week preceding the Pentagon's confirmation.

Following a meeting with Trump and other technology leaders, Amodei stated that risk management discussions were ongoing, remarking that "if we work with the president, we can win safely." Trump subsequently praised Amodei during the appearance, describing the executive as "great" and "fantastic."
