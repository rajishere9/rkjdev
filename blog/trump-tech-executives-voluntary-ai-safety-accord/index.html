Trump and Tech Leaders Sign Voluntary AI Safety Accord

The White House agreement relies on industry self-policing and external audits, accompanied by an executive order rebranding AI as 'Super Intelligence.'

In short

  • President Trump and major tech executives signed the 'Joint Commitment On Frontier Responsibilities,' a voluntary, morally binding safety framework.
  • The agreement outlines four layers of internal controls, third-party audits, and board reviews, but includes no government enforcement mechanisms.
  • Trump signed an executive order directing federal agencies to use the term 'Super Intelligence' (SI) and stop using 'Artificial Intelligence' (AI).
  • Lawmakers, including Sen. Mark Warner, criticized the voluntary framework for allowing tech companies to regulate themselves amid rising safety concerns.

President Donald Trump and leading technology executives signed a voluntary artificial intelligence agreement at the White House on September 29, 2026. Titled the "Joint Commitment On Frontier Responsibilities," the one-page accord establishes a self-regulatory framework for safety testing and risk management as companies train advanced AI models.

Following a White House luncheon, Trump characterized the document as "almost like a constitution" and described it as "morally binding," according to reporting from CBS News. The meeting brought together executives from prominent AI and hardware companies, including Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, Google CEO Sundar Pichai, AMD CEO Lisa Su, and Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora.

The Four Layers of the Safety Accord

The agreement outlines four tiers of safety controls and testing evaluations intended to monitor advanced models during development, as reported by The Guardian:

  • Layer 1: Internal Safety Monitoring: Continuous monitoring during the training phase of AI models.
  • Layer 2: Internal Oversight Teams: Dedicated internal groups tasked with verifying that first-layer monitoring functions properly.
  • Layer 3: External Audits: Independent third-party auditors granted access to evaluate the company's safety controls.
  • Layer 4: Board Reviews: Independent oversight boards designated to review evaluation reports.

The framework does not empower federal regulators or mandate that companies publicly release the findings of their audits. Under the terms of the voluntary commitment, participating firms select their own external evaluators, establish their own oversight boards, and determine whether to publish evaluation results.

Zuckerberg stated during a post-luncheon press conference that the agreement was created to reassure the public. "This is a start and an accord that the whole industry could come to," Zuckerberg said, according to The Guardian.

Federal Rebrand to 'Super Intelligence'

Alongside the voluntary framework, Trump signed an executive order directing all federal departments and agencies to officially replace the term "artificial intelligence" with "Super Intelligence" or "SI." The directive instructs executive branch agencies to cease acknowledging "AI" or "artificial intelligence" in official communications, policy papers, and correspondence, according to The Verge.

Trump previously outlined the terminology shift during an address at the United Nations General Assembly, arguing that the technology is not artificial. At the White House, Trump also announced that he plans to appoint an "AI czar" within days and discussed creating a 10-person committee to oversee technology developments, as detailed by CBS News.

In addition to the policy directives, the administration unveiled America.gov, a citizen-facing chatbot intended to streamline access to federal services. The tool operates on Google's Gemini platform and SpaceXAI's Grok, according to reports from Engadget and TechCrunch.

Reactions and Criticisms Over Voluntary Oversight

The accord arrives following multiple incidents involving AI testing failures, including disclosures that frontier AI agents accessed government websites without human guidance, as reported by Engadget. While some industry leaders, such as Anthropic's Dario Amodei, have voiced support for curbing the pace of frontier model releases, others like Nvidia's Jensen Huang argued at the White House that innovation and safety progress together.

The reliance on self-policing drew immediate criticism from congressional lawmakers. Democratic Senator Mark Warner of Virginia called for binding statutory requirements rather than industry-led pledges, according to CBS News.

"The companies building the most powerful AI systems are warning us that the technology is advancing faster than our safeguards," Warner said in a statement following the meeting. "The president's response? To rename it and tell the companies developing it to regulate themselves."

Questions people ask

What is the Joint Commitment On Frontier Responsibilities?

It is a voluntary, non-binding accord signed by the White House and major AI tech executives establishing four layers of internal safety monitoring, external auditing, and board reviews for advanced AI models.

Which tech executives participated in the White House accord?

Attendees included Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, and Google CEO Sundar Pichai, among others.

What does the executive order on 'Super Intelligence' mandate?

The executive order directs all US federal executive branch departments and agencies to use the terms 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' in non-statutory documents, public communications, and correspondence, and to stop acknowledging the term 'Artificial Intelligence.'
