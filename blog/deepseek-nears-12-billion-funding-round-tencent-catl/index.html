DeepSeek Nears $12B Funding Round Backed by Tencent and CATL

Strong demand following recent model releases could push the round to $15 billion as the startup prepares a massive domestic chip cluster.

Key takeaways

  • DeepSeek is finalizing a funding round of at least 80 billion yuan ($12 billion) that could reach 100 billion yuan ($15 billion).
  • The round targets a valuation of approximately 500 billion yuan ($75 billion) ahead of a planned 2027 initial public offering on Shanghai's STAR Market.
  • Tencent and electric vehicle battery manufacturer CATL are leading commitments, alongside Geely Auto and institutional asset managers.
  • Capital will finance a large data center in Inner Mongolia housing at least 160,000 Huawei AI accelerators running jointly developed software.

Chinese artificial intelligence laboratory DeepSeek is on the verge of closing a major funding round of at least 80 billion yuan (approximately $12 billion), with total commitments potentially expanding to 100 billion yuan ($15 billion). The financing, initially reported by Bloomberg News, is led by Chinese internet conglomerate Tencent Holdings and electric vehicle battery manufacturer Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd. (CATL). The transaction values the Hangzhou-based startup at roughly 500 billion yuan ($75 billion) as it prepares for an initial public offering targeted for early 2027.

DeepSeek initially set out to raise 50 billion yuan, but strong investor interest pushed the target higher, according to reporting by CNBC. The demand accelerated after the laboratory released its latest architecture, including its V4 series and the V4.1 Flash model rolled out in September 2026, which the company stated was designed for greater capability and faster inference.

Expanded Investor Base and Capital Structure

The funding round has attracted interest across corporate treasuries, state-backed investment vehicles, and private equity managers. Alongside Tencent and CATL, automaker Geely Auto and investment management firms Monolith Management and Loyal Valley Capital have committed capital, according to reporting by CNBC. However, DeepSeek has strictly vetted prospective backers, excluding funds pooled from individual retail investors and confining participation primarily to institutional, corporate, and government funds.

The current negotiations follow DeepSeek's first outside financing round, which closed in June 2026 and valued the enterprise at roughly 350 billion yuan ($52 billion to $59 billion), as noted by The Next Web. In that earlier 50 billion yuan round, founder Liang Wenfeng contributed 20 billion yuan of his own capital, while Tencent invested 10 billion yuan and CATL supplied 5 billion yuan, with additional participation from JD.com, NetEase, and China's national AI investment fund.

Investor talks for the current round experienced friction earlier in the year. Fundraising was briefly paused after comments attributed to Liang during a private investor meeting circulated on social media, according to reporting cited by Quartz. Liang acknowledged that Chinese AI laboratories still trailed American competitors in baseline sophistication and remained heavily reliant on Nvidia hardware. Across investor meetings held this year, Liang committed to keeping DeepSeek's AI models open and reaffirmed that he prioritizes advancing toward artificial general intelligence over commercial returns.

Infrastructure Buildup in Inner Mongolia

A substantial portion of the proceeds from the round will fund DeepSeek's compute infrastructure as it seeks alternatives to Western processors. The company is directing capital toward building a massive data center facility in Inner Mongolia, according to Quartz.

DeepSeek plans to deploy at least 160,000 of Huawei Technologies' top-tier AI accelerators at the Inner Mongolia site, reported Seeking Alpha. If fully populated, the facility would represent one of the largest concentrated installations of domestic Chinese AI silicon in existence. DeepSeek has also released software co-developed with Huawei designed for programming Huawei's AI chips.

Corporate backers are also pursuing strategic alignment through the deal. For CATL, participating in the round offers an entry point into the data center power and supply chain infrastructure, according to Quartz. Tencent, meanwhile, has been integrating DeepSeek's technology directly into consumer products. Its subsidiary Tencent Music Entertainment integrated DeepSeek's models into streaming platforms such as QQ Music and Kugou Music for AI songwriting, recommendation engines, and dynamic playlist generation, reported Music Business Worldwide.

Rapid Release Cycles and Path to 2027 IPO

DeepSeek's massive capital injection coincides with an unprecedented acceleration in AI release cadence across Chinese laboratories. Chinese developers published 16 AI models in September 2026 alone, according to Nikkei Asia reporting summarized by AI Weekly. While Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei publicly urged the global technology industry to slow development to manage risks, Chinese laboratories accelerated. The average development interval between model updates across major US and Chinese labs contracted from 125 days down to 44 days between April and September 2026, with DeepSeek shipping model updates every month since July.

Once the latest financing closes, DeepSeek plans to initiate an internal corporate restructuring to prepare for a public listing, according to TechNode. The company has retained CITIC Securities to structure a potential market debut on the Shanghai Stock Exchange's STAR Market in early 2027. Domestic rival Moonshot AI is pursuing a similar timeline, eyeing an early 2027 initial public offering after closing a financing round that valued it at $50 billion.
