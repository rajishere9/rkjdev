Trump Names Intelligence Chief Jay Clayton as White House AI Czar

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will retain his spy chief post while steering the federal government's Super Intelligence Force review.

In short

  • President Donald Trump appointed Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to lead the administration's newly formed Super Intelligence Force.
  • Clayton retains his post overseeing the 18 U.S. intelligence agencies while serving as the White House AI czar.
  • The task force charter establishes a 120-day window to evaluate risks, audit breach reporting, and determine the federal government's role in AI oversight.
  • Leadership includes vice chairs Emil Michael, Scott Kupor, and Andrew Ferguson, alongside cabinet officials and external advisors like David Sacks and Condoleezza Rice.

President Donald Trump has appointed Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to serve as the new White House AI czar, putting the head of the nation's 18 intelligence agencies in charge of a newly formed federal body titled the Super Intelligence Force. The appointment, announced on Sunday via Truth Social, formalizes leadership for the administration's broader push to structure its artificial intelligence strategy around national security and global competitiveness.

The appointment expands upon the administration's initiative detailed in our earlier report on the Super Intelligence Force launch, setting a dual-hatted role for Clayton that combines domestic technological coordination with the management of the U.S. intelligence enterprise. According to The Guardian, Clayton previously served as the chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and as the top federal prosecutor in Manhattan before being confirmed as intelligence chief.

The 120-Day Mandate for the Super Intelligence Force

Under the task force's operating charter, Clayton and his team have a 120-day deadline to assess artificial intelligence risks and opportunities and define the federal government's scope of responsibility. As reported by qz.com, the charter allows the group's operations to be extended beyond four months or transferred directly into an existing government agency once the initial report is completed.

The charter directs the task force to create plans to handle threats enabled by advanced AI while explicitly avoiding overregulation and regulatory capture that could impede industry competition. Additionally, the task force will audit how the federal government receives alerts regarding system breaches, cyber hacks, and model jailbreaks, identifying administrative improvements that can be executed under existing legal authorities.

Clayton framed the urgency of the role around international competition. "The risk of not being first is high," Clayton stated in an interview with The Wall Street Journal, as reported by Nextgov/FCW. "Not being first increases the identified, and unidentified, risks, particularly from our adversaries."

Task Force Leadership and Advisory Structure

The organizational structure of the Super Intelligence Force establishes three primary vice chairs below Clayton to manage engagement across various sectors:

  • Emil Michael, Undersecretary of Defense for Research and Engineering (CTO of the Pentagon), will serve as the primary liaison to AI commercial developers.
  • Scott Kupor, Director of the Office of Personnel Management, will lead outreach to critical infrastructure operators and industry stakeholders.
  • Andrew Ferguson, Chairman of the Federal Trade Commission, will oversee engagement with consumer advocates, privacy organizations, children's advocacy groups, and religious communities.

According to qz.com, senior government members serving on the task force include Vice President JD Vance, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. External advisors participating in the initiative include former Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice, focusing on national security, and venture capitalist David Sacks.

Sacks previously served as the administration's AI and cryptocurrency czar until March 2026, when his tenure ended upon reaching the statutory time limit for special government employees. Sacks continues to serve as co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology.

Regulatory Philosophy and Sector Collaboration

The creation of the task force follows a White House meeting where technology executives signed voluntary commitments regarding in-house model monitoring, third-party audits, and board-level safety disclosures. Signatories included OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, as noted by qz.com.

Administration officials indicated that the private sector will remain the primary driver of risk management, with federal entities playing a supportive, non-directive role. Clayton pointed to financial-industry risk models developed jointly by the SEC and the Federal Reserve as a potential template for working with frontier AI labs.

Reaction to the appointment on Capitol Hill was cautious. Senator Mark Warner, the vice-chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, welcomed Clayton's involvement but raised concerns regarding strategic direction, as reported by The Guardian. Warner stated that the administration has resisted meaningful safeguards and noted that the scope of Clayton's authority regarding safety standards and model evaluations remains undefined.
