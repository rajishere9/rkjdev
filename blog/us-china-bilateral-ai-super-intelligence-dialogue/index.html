US and China Establish Super Intelligence Dialogue and Incident Channel

Following a summit in Washington, the two nations agreed to create a bilateral AI incident hotline and hold regular talks on super intelligence risks.

Key takeaways

  • The US and China agreed to establish the U.S.-China Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue and a dedicated bilateral incident communication channel.
  • Both nations agreed to use the term 'super intelligence' rather than 'artificial intelligence' to describe the applicable emerging technologies.
  • The next round of bilateral dialogue is scheduled to take place by November 2026.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent outlined threat priorities including autonomous agents, cyber operations, and biological weapons proliferation.
  • Top AI industry leaders, including executives from OpenAI and Meta, attended diplomatic events alongside the summit.

The United States and China have formalized a new bilateral framework to manage critical risks in advanced artificial intelligence, agreeing to establish a standing technical dialogue and a dedicated crisis communication channel. Announced on September 26, 2026, following a reciprocal state visit to Washington by Chinese President Xi Jinping, the diplomatic package establishes formal channels between the world's two leading AI powers.

According to an official White House fact sheet, both governments agreed to use the term "super intelligence" (SI) rather than "artificial intelligence" to describe the relevant frontier systems. The newly formed U.S.-China Super Intelligence Dialogue will serve as the primary forum for exchanging views on the risks and benefits of SI, with the next round of discussions scheduled to take place by November 2026.

The SI Incident Hotline and Working-Level Negotiations

Alongside the dialogue, the two nations agreed to set up a bilateral communication channel specifically designed for AI-related incidents. The channel is designed to allow rapid notification and coordination during technical emergencies.

The framework was finalized following extensive preparatory talks in New York between US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng. According to Anadolu Agency, the two officials met for a 12-hour negotiating session at JPMorgan Chase headquarters in Manhattan to establish operational terms, with initial plans pointing to follow-up meetings in Shenzhen, China.

Bessent emphasized the necessity of moving from opacity to transparency between the two AI leaders, according to AI Weekly. The incident mechanism is aimed at mitigating risks associated with uncontrollable autonomous agents, system breakdowns, severe cyber incidents, and non-state proliferation of biological weapons.

Technical Definitions and Policy Tensions

While the agreement creates a structured mechanism for contact, the public documents do not yet outline full technical protocols. The White House fact sheet did not publish an incident classification taxonomy, designated agency owners, or formal reporting timelines for private frontier research labs, meaning operational rules will depend on negotiations during the November 2026 round.

The summit also highlighted differing domestic approaches to AI oversight. President Trump stated publicly that "our guardrail is the DOJ," while President Xi emphasized that both nations share a responsibility to keep AI development "always under human control."

Major technology executives were present during the summit proceedings. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg attended the official state dinner as the bilateral agreements were finalized. In parallel discussions at the UN Security Council, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei argued that international incident-reporting systems are necessary to manage the global impact of advanced models.

Domestically, US officials signaled a firm stance on corporate accountability. In comments reported by Anadolu Agency, Bessent rejected requests from frontier AI developers seeking federal liability immunity, arguing that private labs should not seek legal exemptions while warning of systemic failure risks.

Broader Diplomatic and Trade Context

The AI safety agreements were part of a broader set of bilateral outcomes from the September 2026 summit. According to the White House fact sheet, the two countries operationalized the U.S.-China Board of Trade and the U.S.-China Board of Investment.

The economic consensus included recommendations for favorable tariff treatment on $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction, Chinese commitments to import at least 10 million metric tons of US coal in 2027 and 2028, and continued bilateral discussions regarding critical mineral supply chains.

What is the purpose of the SI incident communication channel?

The channel serves as a direct bilateral hotline intended to provide rapid communication and crisis management during AI-related emergencies, such as autonomous agent failures, cyberattacks, or biological proliferation threats.
