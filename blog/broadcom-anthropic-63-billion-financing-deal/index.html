Broadcom Secures $63 Billion SPV Debt Deal for Anthropic AI Hardware

A Wall Street consortium is using a special purpose vehicle to fund Broadcom XPU accelerators and networking gear for Anthropic starting in 2027.

In short

  • Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi, and Morgan Stanley announced a $63 billion financing transaction on October 26, 2026, to fund AI compute capacity for Anthropic.
  • A newly created special purpose vehicle will buy Broadcom XPU accelerators and networking hardware, then lease the equipment to Anthropic starting in calendar 2027.
  • The debt is split between senior first-lien financing arranged by banks and second-lien financing provided by Blackstone, backed by residual value support from Broadcom.
  • The transaction coincides with Anthropic filing for an initial public offering on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

On October 26, 2026, financial giants Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi, and Morgan Stanley announced a landmark $63 billion financing package designed to deploy artificial intelligence compute infrastructure using Broadcom technology for Anthropic. According to Broadcom's official announcement, the transaction forms part of an earlier strategic compute capacity agreement, with hardware deliveries expected to begin in calendar year 2027.

The massive debt package comes at a critical juncture for Anthropic. Earlier on October 26, the company officially filed for an initial public offering under the ticker ANT on the Nasdaq, with reporting from The Wall Street Journal cited by Morningstar indicating the listing could value the AI lab between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion.

How the Special Purpose Vehicle Is Structured

To finance the capital-intensive infrastructure buildout, the partners established a newly formed special purpose vehicle (SPV). The SPV will purchase Broadcom XPU AI accelerators and networking solutions, which will subsequently be leased to Anthropic.

The financing package divides risk across senior debt, alternative credit, and Broadcom itself. According to statements published by Blackstone, Bank of America, Citi, and Morgan Stanley are arranging the first-lien portion of the credit package. Blackstone is serving as the sole provider of the committed portion of the second-lien financing.

Sources familiar with the financing told Reuters via KSL that the first-lien senior debt accounts for approximately $43.5 billion, or roughly 69 percent of the total $63 billion sum. To support the credit structure, Broadcom is providing residual value support to the SPV in an amount equivalent to the first-lien senior debt, creating a backstop that absorbs potential declines in hardware asset value.

Advisory and legal teams involved in the transaction include Kirkland & Ellis LLP as legal counsel to Blackstone, Latham & Watkins LLP representing the bank syndicate, Sullivan & Cromwell LLP representing Broadcom, and Evercore serving as Broadcom's financial advisor.

Scaling AI Compute Capacity for Anthropic

The specialized hardware procured through the SPV consists of Broadcom XPU AI accelerators and networking solutions. While deliveries to the SPV begin in 2027, the arrangement deepens previous capacity agreements between Broadcom and Anthropic.

Anthropic has been aggressively lining up computational infrastructure through multiple avenues. In addition to hardware commitments with Broadcom, analysis from Dealroom.co notes that Anthropic has also secured compute agreements with Microsoft, Nscale, and SpaceX to meet expanding workload demands.

This is also not the first time alternative asset managers have financed Anthropic compute. A prior agreement between Broadcom, Blackstone, and Apollo saw the two firms agree to finance a $35 billion capacity expansion, where Broadcom provided backstop guarantees of up to $29 billion according to regulatory filings cited by Reuters.

"The rapidly growing demand for AI is creating an enormous opportunity to finance compute capacity at scale," Blackstone President and COO Jon Gray said in the joint release. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan highlighted that the deal "validates that there is capital available to finance such growth and that Broadcom is uniquely positioned to enable it."

Shifting the Financial Burden of AI Hardware

The sheer size of the $63 billion package highlights the expanding role of institutional credit and private asset managers in absorbing the upfront capital expenditures of AI expansion. Data compiled by Dealroom.co indicates that the package ranks in the top 1 percent of United States telecom post-IPO debt transactions recorded across 94 comparable rounds over the past four years.

However, the rapid accumulation of debt-backed computing assets carries potential structural hazards. Credit rating agency Moody's, while describing Broadcom as one of the strongest technology firms, warned that credit risk could mount if multiple large-scale financing transactions are undertaken in short intervals to satisfy the enormous funding requirements of frontier labs, as reported by KSL.

Following the announcement, financial markets showed measured movement. Reporting from Investing.com noted that Broadcom shares gained 0.6 percent in after-hours trading on October 26, while Blackstone shares edged up toward $119.06. Because the financing funds an already disclosed hardware agreement rather than a newly signed customer deployment, market reaction remained orderly as the industry adjusts to multi-billion-dollar synthetic leasing structures.

Which financial institutions are participating in the transaction?

Bank of America, Citi, and Morgan Stanley are arranging the senior first-lien financing, while alternative asset manager Blackstone is acting as the sole committed provider of the second-lien financing.

What is Broadcom's financial exposure in the SPV?

Broadcom is providing residual value support to the SPV equivalent to the value of the first-lien financing portion, estimated by sources cited by Reuters to be approximately $43.5 billion.

When will Anthropic receive the Broadcom computing hardware?

According to official announcements from Broadcom and Blackstone, deliveries of the AI compute hardware are scheduled to begin in calendar year 2027.
