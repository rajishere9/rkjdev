Aramco Buys 49% Stake in AI Firm HUMAIN for $2.6 Billion

Aramco and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund team up to scale HUMAIN into an AI powerhouse targeting 3 gigawatts of compute capacity by 2030.

Saudi Aramco and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) finalized a transaction on November 1, 2026, granting Aramco a 49% stake in artificial intelligence firm HUMAIN. Aramco completed the acquisition through a wholly owned subsidiary by contributing approximately SAR 9.9 billion ($2.6 billion) in cash during 2026, as well as transferring AI, compute, and digital assets. PIF retains the remaining 51% majority ownership.

The closing formalizes an agreement that began with a non-binding term sheet signed on October 28, 2025, according to announcements by Aramco and PIF. Under the agreement, both shareholders will oversee HUMAIN's international expansion and contribute AI assets, technical capabilities, and talent to build an integrated technology stack.

Transaction Details and Asset Transfers

The deal combines state investment backing with Aramco's computing and operational resources. According to statements published on Aramco's newsroom and reported by Arab News, the agreement involves transferring core computing infrastructure and digital programs from Aramco to HUMAIN. This follows an earlier consolidation step in which the Saudi Company for Artificial Intelligence (SCAI), previously wholly owned by PIF, was transferred to HUMAIN, as reported by Unite.AI.

Yazeed A. Al-Humied, Deputy Governor and Head of MENA Investments at PIF, said combining the AI investment portfolios under HUMAIN creates synergies in talent development, technological progress, and intellectual property creation. Al-Humied noted that the transaction aligns with the PIF 2026–2030 strategy to integrate economic ecosystems, broaden private sector participation, and build globally competitive domestic companies.

For Aramco, the investment complements its core energy business while opening avenues for commercial returns. Aramco said that it and its affiliates expect to benefit from access to HUMAIN's computing systems and technical services, creating opportunities for revenue growth, healthy operating margins, and enhanced monetization of its existing digital assets.

HUMAIN's Infrastructure and 3-Gigawatt Target

HUMAIN launched in May 2025 to develop full-stack artificial intelligence services across four distinct areas:

  • Next-generation data centers
  • High-performance infrastructure and cloud platforms
  • Advanced foundation models, including ALLaM, an Arabic multimodal large language model
  • Industry-focused software and operational AI solutions

The company is working toward deploying 3 gigawatts (GW) of AI data center capacity by 2030. The joint statements describe this capacity target as an effort to meet rising domestic and international compute demand and position HUMAIN as an exporter of AI capabilities.

HUMAIN has already begun committing portions of its planned infrastructure to international customers. On October 21, 2026, HUMAIN and infrastructure firm DataVolt announced a preliminary framework agreement with AI cloud provider Nebius, according to reporting by Arab News. Under that framework, Nebius agreed to take up to 50 megawatts of capacity at a planned campus located in Neom's Oxagon industrial complex. The allocation accounts for half of the initial 100 megawatts under construction within the project's 360-megawatt first phase, with phased operations scheduled to start in the fourth quarter of 2027.

Industrial Applications and Aramco's Balance Sheet

Aramco plans to run industrial applications across HUMAIN's infrastructure to improve operations across the hydrocarbon chain. Aramco President and CEO Amin H. Nasser highlighted the company's prior financial return from deploying digital systems across its energy assets.

"Since 2023, Aramco has achieved $11.3 billion in Technology Realized Value, with a significant portion coming from AI solutions," Nasser said in the closing announcement reported by Arabian Business. Nasser added that the partnership with PIF establishes a base for localizing the technology sector and speeds up integrated, scalable AI deployments.

Reporting from Gulf News highlights that the cash injection of roughly SAR 9.9 billion will occur over the course of 2026.

Why the Deal Matters

By acquiring a 49% stake through a wholly owned subsidiary rather than acting simply as a enterprise customer, Aramco secures equity ownership in critical digital infrastructure.

The transaction also consolidates Saudi Arabia's state-backed AI assets under a single operational umbrella. By bringing together PIF's investment capital, SCAI's previous portfolio, and Aramco's computing capacity and industrial footprint, HUMAIN enters 2027 with substantial domestic backing as it builds out its initial data center facilities at Neom and expands its multimodal Arabic large language models such as ALLaM.
