OpenAI Revenue Run Rate Approaches $70 Billion on Enterprise Surge

Enterprise revenue has doubled since July and consumer adoption has surged, pushing OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate toward $70 billion.

Key takeaways

  • OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate is approaching $70 billion, jumping more than 70% since the start of Q3 2026.
  • Enterprise sales have surged over 100% since July 2026, while Q3 consumer revenue surpassed total consumer sales from the entire year of 2025.
  • Oracle stock climbed 5.3% on the news, driven by OpenAI representing roughly half of Oracle's compute backlog.
  • Both OpenAI and rival Anthropic are preparing for potential initial public offerings while handling massive infrastructure costs and safety reviews.

OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate is approaching $70 billion, driven by rapid adoption from corporate clients and strong consumer demand. The artificial intelligence developer has seen its annualized run rate surge by more than 70% since the start of the third quarter in July 2026, according to financial reporting first detailed by Axios and confirmed by Reuters via KSL.

The sudden acceleration reflects commercial expansion across both business and direct consumer channels. Business-to-business sales have increased by more than 100% since July, while consumer revenue collected in the third quarter of 2026 alone surpassed the company's total consumer revenue for the entirety of 2025.

Enterprise Adoption Drives the OpenAI Revenue Run Rate

The company's revenue growth gained significant velocity midway through the year. According to reporting from Quartz, OpenAI privately informed investors that commercial adoption quickened following the release of new models in July 2026. Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar previously notified employees that the company's annualized recurring revenue in July had already outpaced its total revenue for the second quarter.

Leadership changes in the sales division accompanied the commercial expansion. Chief Revenue Officer Dali Rajic joined OpenAI around July 2026, replacing Denise Dresser, who stepped down after less than a year in the role.

Run rate figures extrapolate short-term performance over a 12-month horizon, a calculation standard among high-growth technology companies that can also amplify brief revenue spikes. Bloomberg News reported in August that OpenAI was initially tracking toward an annualized run rate exceeding $40 billion, approximately double its rate at the close of 2025, before growth accelerated further into September.

The Commercial Battle Between OpenAI and Anthropic

The latest figures show OpenAI regaining ground in a tight race against key rival Anthropic. In the second quarter of 2026, Anthropic surpassed OpenAI in quarterly revenue for the first time, recording $11.6 billion compared to OpenAI's $6.7 billion, according to Quartz.

Anthropic saw its full-year 2025 revenue increase twelvefold to nearly $4.6 billion, and by July 2026, its annualized revenue reached approximately $65 billion. Both companies are currently laying the groundwork for initial public offerings, having filed confidential paperwork to list shares publicly, as reported by PYMNTS. Anthropic may list as early as autumn 2026, whereas OpenAI has indicated it will not pursue an IPO this year.

Compute Obligations and Model Safety Roadblocks

Despite surging top-line numbers, both leading model builders carry immense capital requirements. Audited financial documents indicate OpenAI posted a net loss of $38.5 billion in 2025 on $13.07 billion in revenue. Details regarding OpenAI's 2026 operating expenses remain undisclosed.

Infrastructure providers stand to benefit directly from this compute demand. Shares of Oracle gained 5.3% following the revenue reports. "Oracle's fate is largely tied to the success of OpenAI, which represents around half of its compute backlog," said Gil Luria, managing director at equity research firm D.A. Davidson, in remarks to Reuters. Anthropic faces similar capital scale, having reported a $42 billion net loss in 2025 alongside plans to spend at least $518 billion over a decade on cloud and hardware partnerships.

Alongside financial considerations, deployment safety remains a central operational focus. OpenAI recently halted the planned rollout of its GPT-6.1 Astra model after safety evaluations showed performance regressions. Saachi Jain, OpenAI's head of safety, explained to the Wall Street Journal that the model tested poorly on alignment evaluations, highlighting the ongoing technical balance between task autonomy and strict compliance as developers prepare future architectures for enterprise deployment.
