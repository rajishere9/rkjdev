Sony Brings AI Upscaling to Standard PlayStation 5 With QSSR

Developed alongside AMD under Project Amethyst, Quick Spectral Super Resolution brings machine learning image reconstruction to standard PS5 hardware.

Key takeaways

  • Sony introduced Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR), a lightweight AI upscaler tailored for the base PS5 hardware.
  • The technology originated from Project Amethyst, an engineering collaboration between Sony and AMD.
  • Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei are the first titles receiving patches to support QSSR graphics modes.
  • PS5 system software version 26.06-14.10.00 is available to download now alongside the announcement of QSSR.

Sony Interactive Entertainment has rolled out artificial intelligence upscaling to the standard PlayStation 5, expanding a visual reconstruction technique that was previously exclusive to the higher-end PlayStation 5 Pro. Announced in an official PlayStation.Blog post, the feature arrives as Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR), a specialized performance tier of machine learning upscaling designed to operate within the computational budget of the base console.

Console games frequently render internally at lower native resolutions before outputting to display panels. Sony says its new pipeline uses AI to upscale frames with more detail and stability than conventional approaches.

Technical Architecture and Project Amethyst

QSSR represents a streamlined derivative of PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR), which originally launched on the PlayStation 5 Pro. According to Sony, the heavier computational requirements of full PSSR prevented the original implementation from running on standard PS5 silicon.

To overcome those processing constraints, Sony collaborated with AMD on an initiative called Project Amethyst, as detailed by PlayStation.Blog. The initiative yielded two primary modifications: a streamlined neural network architecture and a hand-tuned implementation tailored directly to maximize performance on the base PS5.

"We are bringing the benefits of AI-guided upscaling to PS5. While PSSR remains the gold standard on PS5 Pro, QSSR provides some notable improvements to both image detail and image stability on PS5," stated Daniel Craig, Principal Engineer of Graphics R&D at PlayStation, in the announcement.

As detailed by FlatpanelsHD, PS5 system software version 26.06-14.10.00 is available to download now.

Initial Game Support and Developer Integration

Because QSSR requires specific engine integration, existing games cannot automatically apply the technology without targeted updates. Sony confirmed that the first two titles supporting the new graphics option are Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei, with patches deploying immediately.

Engineers at both development studios highlighted notable improvements in visual fidelity. Mike Fitzgerald, Head of Technology at Insomniac Games, noted that the integration "adds more pixel-level clarity and stability to our environments, bringing out extra detail amidst the busy streets of Madripoor, the dense alleyways of Shinjuku, and many other areas across Logan's epic journey," according to Engadget.

Jasmin Patry, Lead Rendering Engineer at Sucker Punch Productions, shared similar findings regarding Ghost of Yōtei. Patry explained that "QSSR excels at resolving the fine details in our characters and environments and does so with a level of temporal stability that hasn’t been possible on the PS5 console until now." The update arrives alongside a complete edition release for the title featuring a story expansion and a roguelike mode, with existing owners able to upgrade for $15.

Sony Interactive Entertainment confirmed that the QSSR developer library is being distributed broadly to all PlayStation developers, allowing studios to incorporate the upscaler into future releases or retroactively patch existing software.

Broader Console Updates and Platform Strategy

Bringing machine learning upscaling to older base hardware deviates from standard console lifecycle practices, where major rendering upgrades remain locked to mid-generation hardware refreshes. As noted by GamesRadar+, QSSR operates purely as an image reconstruction tool aimed at sharpening rendered pixels, rather than generating or altering artistic assets.

Alongside the software enhancements, Sony has also quietly introduced a hardware revision for newly manufactured standard PlayStation 5 units. Teardowns from independent hardware modders cited by FlatpanelsHD show that the revised chassis features an updated motherboard, a replaceable CR2032 battery, and a cooling system modeled after the PS5 Pro, marking ongoing maintenance for the base platform.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between PSSR and QSSR?

PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR) is Sony's full-scale AI upscaler designed for the more powerful PS5 Pro. Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR) is a streamlined, hand-tuned neural network developed with AMD to run within the computational limits of the standard PS5.

Which games currently support QSSR on the base PS5?

The first games to receive patches enabling QSSR are Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei, with Sony making the library available for other developers to adopt.

Do games automatically use QSSR without a patch?

No. Games must be updated or built from the ground up by developers to integrate the QSSR library, even if they already support PSSR on the PS5 Pro.
