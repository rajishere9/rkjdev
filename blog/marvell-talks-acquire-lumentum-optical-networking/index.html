Marvell in Advanced Talks to Buy Optical Supplier Lumentum, Valued at $83 Billion

The potential combination would unite Marvell's custom silicon portfolio with Lumentum's optical laser technology to ease data transfer bottlenecks in AI clusters.

In short

  • Marvell Technology is in advanced talks to acquire optical component manufacturer Lumentum Holdings, which has a market capitalization of roughly $83 billion.
  • A final agreement could be reached as early as next week, though discussions remain private and terms could still shift.
  • The acquisition would bring Lumentum's laser and high-speed optical manufacturing directly into Marvell's data center networking portfolio.
  • Lumentum shares rose between 8.5% and 17% in extended trading on October 28, 2026, while Marvell shares dropped more than 7% to 9.3%.

Chipmaker Marvell Technology is in advanced discussions to acquire optical equipment supplier Lumentum Holdings, which has an $83 billion market capitalization, though transaction terms and valuation have not been finalized, according to a report from Bloomberg cited by Investing.com. If completed, the transaction would rank among the semiconductor sector's largest combinations of the year and solidify Marvell's grip on optical hardware critical for artificial intelligence infrastructure.

According to people familiar with the matter, an agreement between the two Northern California companies could be announced as soon as next week, though discussions remain confidential, terms may shift, and no final transaction has been signed. Representatives for both companies did not immediately respond to requests for comment, Benzinga and MarketWatch reported.

Market Reactions to the Potential Combination

Investors responded swiftly during extended trading on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. Shares of Lumentum Holdings jumped sharply on the takeover news, advancing 8.54% to $991.99 in Benzinga Pro data, surging more than 9% on Investing.com, and climbing as high as 13% to $1,040.00 according to Dow Jones Newswires. At one point, Lumentum stock traded up 17% after hours.

Conversely, shares of Marvell Technology retreated as investors weighed the financial scale and integration risks of a takeover of Lumentum, which has an $83 billion market capitalization, though transaction terms and valuation have not been finalized. Marvell's stock dropped 8.37% to $230.59 in after-hours trading on Benzinga Pro and slid 9.3% to $228.31 according to Dow Jones data, after closing the regular session up 3.3% at $251.65. Marvell carried a market valuation of roughly $221 billion prior to the after-hours dip.

Solving Data Bottlenecks with Optical Networking

Lumentum manufactures specialized lasers and high-speed optical components that transmit large volumes of data across data centers and telecom networks with low latency. In modern computing facilities, optical hardware uses light rather than electrical signals across traditional copper wiring to shuttle information between server racks.

As clusters for generative artificial intelligence expand, transferring data quickly between thousands of connected processors has emerged as a major physical bottleneck, as outlined by Investing.com. While Marvell is widely recognized for producing custom silicon and data center processors, it already operates an optical unit and purchases lasers directly from Lumentum. Buying Lumentum outright would bring laser fabrication and optical manufacturing in-house, giving Marvell end-to-end control over hardware it currently procures from third-party suppliers.

Expanding Marvell's Infrastructure Portfolio

The talks follow a sequence of major optical and custom silicon bets across the semiconductor industry. Last year, Marvell reached an agreement to acquire startup Celestial AI for $3.25 billion to secure its photonics technology, which relies on light to create inter-chip connections, Benzinga reported. In August 2026, a regulatory filing indicated that Alphabet could purchase up to $12.2 billion in Marvell shares at $206.58 per share under a multi-year custom-chip partnership spanning through fiscal 2033.

Lumentum has also drawn significant commercial backing from other major hardware builders. In March 2026, Nvidia agreed to invest $2 billion each into Lumentum and optical component peer Coherent. That arrangement included multibillion-dollar purchase commitments and guaranteed Nvidia future capacity and access to advanced laser systems. Following that agreement, Lumentum Chief Executive Officer Michael Hurlston stated that the company would construct a new fabrication facility to expand manufacturing capacity.

Integration Outlook and Regulatory Hurdles

If concluded, absorbing Lumentum would expand Marvell's footprint across both digital silicon and physical optics, creating a unified supplier for hyperscale cloud operators and AI data center builders. However, market observers cited by Investing.com noted that closing an acquisition of this magnitude would likely require navigating substantial regulatory scrutiny and complex integration steps before any transaction can be completed.
