Meta Builds Max Humanoid Robot Prototype with Fourier

Built in roughly three months, the experimental humanoid serves as a testbed for Meta's Muse AI agent as engineering begins on Max 2.

In short

  • Meta partnered with Shanghai-based Fourier to build Max, a five-foot-tall, 106-pound humanoid prototype, in approximately three months.
  • The robot runs on Nvidia Jetson Thor chips and features five-fingered hands, a facial display screen, and integrated speakers.
  • Internally, Meta views humanoid hardware as the physical embodiment of its Muse AI agent for household chores.
  • Work on an upgraded prototype called Max 2 began in September 2026, though Meta states the project remains strictly internal research with no sales plans.

Meta has developed a prototype humanoid robot codenamed Max in partnership with Shanghai-based robotics manufacturer Fourier, according to a report from The Information cited by The Next Web and Engadget. The experimental machine was constructed in roughly three months and unveiled to Meta staff at an internal robotics summit in late August 2026. Meta confirmed to The Information that the project serves internal research and experimentation purposes, with no current launch date or commercial release planned.

Meta views humanoid robotics as a path toward deploying physical artificial intelligence in domestic settings. The initiative aligns directly with the company's efforts to provide an embodied form for its internal AI systems, exploring how machine intelligence can manage everyday household chores.

Hardware Specifications and Design of the Max Prototype

The Max prototype stands approximately 1.5 meters (five feet) tall and weighs roughly 48 kilograms (106 pounds). Designed to test domestic manipulation, the robot is equipped with five-fingered hands engineered to pick up small objects, an integrated speaker system, and an external display screen capable of mimicking facial expressions.

Under the hood, Meta selected Nvidia's Jetson Thor computing platform to run the robot's onboard processing, as reported by The Next Web. Development involved deep physical collaboration between teams: Li-Chen Miller, Meta's vice president of product for robotics, spent almost two months at Fourier's factory in Shanghai to work with Fourier.

Embodying the Muse AI Agent

Inside Meta, the push into physical robotics is led by the team housed within Meta Superintelligence Labs. Meta Chief Technology Officer Andrew Bosworth has described humanoid robots internally as the intended physical form for Muse, Meta's AI agent, according to reporting by Shopifreaks.

The company has steadily assembled talent and corporate backing to support this embodied AI vision. In May 2026, Meta acquired Assured Robot Intelligence, a San Diego-based startup specializing in artificial intelligence software for robotic platforms, integrating its engineering team directly into its robotics group. Hardware collaboration has also expanded beyond manufacturing: Nvidia agreed in late October 2026 to invest $3 billion in Meta while broadening joint initiatives surrounding the Muse architecture, as detailed by The Next Web.

Max 2 Development and Regulatory Pressures

Even before details of the initial prototype became public, engineering work had already shifted to a successor. Meta and Fourier began developing a second iteration, codenamed Max 2, in September 2026. According to Engadget, the Max 2 design introduces a re-engineered torso, improved hand mechanisms, an integrated projector, and supplementary navigation sensors designed to help the robot handle stairs better.

Despite the rapid engineering progress, cross-border hardware development faces mounting hurdles. In August 2026, the Federal Communications Commission proposed reducing the number of Chinese-made robotic units that companies can import for testing purposes without formal written approval from 4,000 units to just 40, as reported by Shopifreaks. Meta acknowledged experiencing the same supply chain challenges as peer companies when attempting to source specialized robotic components outside the United States, emphasizing that its collaboration with Fourier remains an internal research exercise rather than a consumer product rollout.

Questions people ask

What is Meta's Max robot?

Max is an internal prototype humanoid robot built by Meta in collaboration with Chinese robotics firm Fourier. It stands 1.5 meters tall, weighs 106 pounds, and is designed to test household chore capabilities.

What hardware powers the Max prototype?

The robot runs on Nvidia's Jetson Thor computing platform and includes five-fingered hands, a digital facial expression display, and built-in speakers.

Can consumers purchase the Max humanoid robot?

No. Meta stated that the Fourier collaboration is strictly for internal research and experimentation, with no current launch date or plans to sell units.
