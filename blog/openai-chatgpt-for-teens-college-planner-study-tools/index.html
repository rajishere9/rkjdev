OpenAI Announces College Planner Coming Soon and Rolls Out New Study Tools to ChatGPT for…

The update introduces automated flashcard generation and partnerships with youth advising groups, while college admissions tracking through College Planner will be added soon.

In short

  • OpenAI announced College Planner for ChatGPT for Teens to organize application timelines, deadlines, and financial aid tasks.
  • New study features include iOS multi-photo note scanning to PDF, automated flashcard generation, and interactive chat quizzes.
  • OpenAI reported that teenagers spend an average of under 15 minutes per day on ChatGPT, with less than 2% engaging in sessions longer than three hours.
  • The rollout comes amid youth safety scrutiny and includes partnerships with College Advising Corps and Boston Children’s Hospital.

On October 7, 2026, OpenAI announced that it will soon roll out College Planner within ChatGPT for Teens, expanding the youth-oriented tier with centralized application trackers, financial aid guidance, and new exam prep features. The announcement details dedicated studying tools, including multi-page note scanning and automated flashcards, alongside usage statistics from the teen tier first deployed on August 18, 2026.

According to an official post on the OpenAI announcement page, hundreds of thousands of high school students in the United States already use the chatbot weekly to research institutions, organize materials, and parse admissions steps. The company is positioning College Planner to manage practical logistics without making qualitative enrollment choices on behalf of applicants.

Organizing College Applications and Financial Aid

College Planner consolidates deadlines, institutional requirements, specific application tasks, and financial aid steps for an applicant's chosen schools into a unified timeline. Students applying to multiple institutions can update their progress, track remaining obligations, and prompt the system with procedural questions regarding confusing forms or application criteria, as reported by The Verge.

The tool also focuses on the financial mechanics of higher education. High schoolers can review requirements for financial aid, monitor filing deadlines across different universities, and explore options for scholarships and fee waivers. OpenAI stated that the initial release is designed for US high school students in grades 10 through 12 applying to four-year undergraduate programs. The company plans to expand support over time to two-year colleges, trade schools, technical programs, and international students.

Multi-Photo Capture, Flashcards, and Quizzes

Beyond application management, OpenAI introduced several study workflows aimed at reducing friction when uploading handwritten schoolwork. As detailed by Digital Trends, the mobile app now includes continuous multi-photo capture for iOS devices, allowing students to photograph multiple pages of notes and combine them into a single PDF before uploading. OpenAI indicated that Android support for this feature is in development.

Once notes are uploaded or a topic is selected, ChatGPT can generate flashcards that save directly to a student's Library. Users can review decks, mark concepts they have mastered, filter items they missed, and shuffle decks for repeated practice. Students can also request that ChatGPT schedule reminder tasks to practice specific sets.

Interactive quizzes have also received updates. OpenAI says the system is better at recognizing when a user wants a quiz, letting students test comprehension directly in chat sessions based on uploaded class materials.

Usage Metrics and Youth Safety Scrutiny

Alongside the tool releases, OpenAI published engagement data for ChatGPT for Teens, noting that ChatGPT has 1.2 billion users globally, addressing concerns surrounding teen screen time and academic dependency. The company disclosed that teenagers on the platform spend an average of under 15 minutes per day on ChatGPT. Less than 2% of teen users log sessions exceeding three consecutive hours, and OpenAI noted that among teens with sessions longer than three consecutive hours, over 80% had at least one prompt related to learning, according to Unite.AI.

The teen experience also incorporates automatic break reminders. OpenAI reported that in almost half of teen conversations with a break reminder, teens paused or ended their conversation within five minutes. OpenAI noted that teens enrolled in the teen tier sent an average of 2.7 million more learning-related messages than those without access, with popular starter prompts including "ask questions to develop my ideas," "give feedback on my draft," and "help me check my evidence." According to OpenAI, nearly 1.2 million teens used Learning Visualizations for math and science in a single week, while more than 180,000 used Study Mode.

The feature expansion arrives following scrutiny from child advocacy groups. A recent evaluation by Common Sense Media designated ChatGPT for Teens an "unacceptable risk," testing the system across 4,000 prompts and alleging that it fails to alert parents appropriately and still completes homework assignments for students.

Advising Partnerships and Student Councils

To complement its automated college planning tools, OpenAI announced support for two youth-focused initiatives. The company is funding College Advising Corps, a nonprofit organization that places recent college graduates as near-peer advisers in under-resourced secondary schools. According to Unite.AI, the organization operates with more than 300 advisers in 2026, working against an average US ratio of 376 students to one counselor. OpenAI's contribution will finance expansion into new states and establish an AI fellowship program where advisers test methods to integrate AI into college navigation.

OpenAI also pledged three years of support to the Digital Wellness Lab at Boston Children’s Hospital and its independent Student Advisory Council. For the 2026–27 academic year, the Lab will assemble approximately 22 students across two cohorts: 12 students aged 14 to 18 in the "Changemakers" community wellness track, and 10 students aged 16 to 20 in the "Architects" technology track. Participants will test AI features directly, providing feedback on safety defaults, parental notifications, and AI literacy to inform model design decisions.

Questions people ask

Who can access College Planner in ChatGPT?

The tool is launching first in the United States for high school students in grades 10 through 12 who plan to attend four-year colleges. Support for two-year programs, trade schools, and international regions is planned for later updates.

What study tools were added to ChatGPT for Teens?

OpenAI added continuous multi-photo note capture to generate PDFs on iOS, automated flashcard generation with library storage and review reminders, and improved interactive quiz creation in chat.

How much time do teenagers spend on ChatGPT?

According to OpenAI, teens spend an average of under 15 minutes per day on ChatGPT, with fewer than 2% engaging in sessions longer than three consecutive hours.
