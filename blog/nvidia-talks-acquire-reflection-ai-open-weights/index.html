Nvidia Explores Potential Deal to Acquire Open-Model Startup Reflection AI

Early discussions between the chipmaker and the $25 billion open-weights startup could take the form of an acqui-hire or expanded equity stake.

rkj dev newsroom Oct 11, 2026 2 min read 2 sources, fact-checked

Share

In short

  • Nvidia is in early talks to acquire, acqui-hire, or expand its investment in open-weights startup Reflection AI.
  • Reflection AI was valued at $25 billion during a March funding round, with Nvidia already holding an $800 million stake.
  • Potential structures under review include an acqui-hire to license technology and bring in staff while sidestepping antitrust scrutiny.
  • Sources caution that negotiations could result in a deal in the coming weeks or fall through entirely.

Nvidia is in early discussions to acquire or deepen its stake in Reflection AI, a U.S. startup developing open-weight artificial intelligence models, according to a report published on October 10, 2026, by the Financial Times via Seeking Alpha.

The discussions remain at an early stage, and deal terms have not been finalized, according to people familiar with the matter cited by the Financial Times via TradingView. Reflection AI was valued at $25 billion in a March funding round. Nvidia already holds an $800 million investment in the startup, and the parties are exploring several pathways forward, ranging from a direct acquisition to alternate operational structures.

Potential Deal Structures and Regulatory Considerations

The structure of any final agreement could take multiple forms to balance strategic alignment and regulatory hurdles. According to reporting from TradingView, one option under consideration is an "acqui-hire." Under such an arrangement, Nvidia would hire key Reflection AI personnel and secure intellectual property licensing rights rather than executing a standard corporate takeover.

An acqui-hire allows tech giants to hire staff and license technology, avoiding antitrust scrutiny linked to a full acquisition. People familiar with the situation noted that talks could lead to an agreement in the coming weeks, though either party could still walk away from negotiations without an agreement.

Nvidia's Growing Open-Weights Strategy

Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang has previously called for the expansion of an open-weight AI ecosystem.

Beyond a personnel absorption or full purchase, the Financial Times via Seeking Alpha reported that Nvidia could also decide to make an additional equity investment in Reflection AI or formulate an agreement to supply increased amounts of compute power and specialized chips to the startup. Neither Nvidia nor Reflection AI responded to requests for comment regarding the discussions.
