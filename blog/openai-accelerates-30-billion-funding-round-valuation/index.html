OpenAI Accelerates $30B Funding Round at $1.4T Valuation

The ChatGPT maker is pushing to secure capital from SoftBank, MGX, and sovereign funds before competitor Anthropic lists shares in mid-November.

In short

  • OpenAI is negotiating an unusually fast $30 billion funding round seeking a valuation above $1.4 trillion.
  • The push aims to lock in capital from sovereign wealth and long-only funds ahead of Anthropic's mid-November 2026 initial public offering.
  • OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue reached $50 billion following the launch of GPT-6 Astra and is projected to hit $70 billion by year-end.
  • A methodological dispute has emerged, with OpenAI accusing Anthropic of inflating revenue by recording gross third-party cloud sales rather than net revenue.
  • Market volatility, bond yields, and heavy data center debt loads have triggered listing pullbacks across the broader tech and infrastructure sector.

OpenAI is moving rapidly to secure commitments for a funding round of roughly $30 billion that would value the developer at more than $1.4 trillion, according to reporting by Reuters. The unusually fast fundraising drive is aimed at closing agreements with institutional backers before its chief competitor, Anthropic, completes a planned initial public offering in mid-November 2026. With both frontier artificial intelligence laboratories seeking capital from an overlapping pool of sovereign wealth and long-only funds, the aggressive timetable is intended to secure resources before public market disclosures enable direct financial comparisons between the two companies.

Talks for the multi-billion-dollar round build on preliminary discussions first reported in early October, as detailed by The Next Web. SoftBank Group and Abu Dhabi-based investment vehicle MGX, both existing backers of OpenAI, are in negotiations to participate in the new financing. OpenAI conducted meetings with sovereign wealth funds and long-only institutional investors last week, coinciding directly with an investor day hosted by Anthropic. Both OpenAI and SoftBank declined to comment on the talks, while Anthropic and MGX did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The Race Against Anthropic's Public Listing

The compressed timeline reflects a strategic push to capture institutional allocations before Anthropic lists its shares. Anthropic had authoritatively led OpenAI in valuation since May 2026, according to Gizmodo. However, investor sentiment has shifted following Anthropic's third-quarter financial results. While Anthropic informed investors that its quarterly revenue expanded to $16.8 billion from $11.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, slowing month-to-month revenue growth led prospective and existing investors to argue for a debut valuation near $1.5 trillion, down from Anthropic's initial target of $2 trillion.

Edward Best, co-chair of capital markets at Willkie Farr & Gallagher, told Reuters that OpenAI likely wants to establish its valuation independently rather than be judged against Anthropic's listing metrics. Best noted that the situation represents a two-horse race in terms of entering the public market.

OpenAI was last valued at $852 billion in March 2026 following a $122 billion funding round that included a commitment from Amazon to invest up to $50 billion, according to Quartz. Although OpenAI confidentially filed an IPO prospectus in June 2026, chief executive Sam Altman ruled out a debut during 2026. Altman subsequently indicated that OpenAI would not go public until its models stop "going rogue," with the organization currently targeting an IPO in 2027.

Revenue Trajectories and Model Economics

To justify the $1.4 trillion price tag, OpenAI is pitching investors on sharp revenue acceleration driven by its recent model releases. The company informed prospective backers that its annualized revenue reached $50 billion following the release of its GPT-6 Astra model, according to sources cited by CTV News. OpenAI told investors it expects annualized revenue to expand to $70 billion by the end of 2026. The lab also reported that price cuts implemented across its less expensive models drove higher customer usage, and told investors it can further lower the price of AI models.

By comparison, Anthropic's annualized revenue stood at approximately $65 billion by July 2026. The differing figures have fueled a debate over revenue recognition methodologies. OpenAI records sales on a net basis, subtracting partner revenue shares upfront before booking the total. It has accused Anthropic of inflating headline revenue by booking the full gross value of enterprise contracts distributed through third-party cloud platforms. In response, Anthropic told Reuters that its accounting adheres strictly to established industry standards.

Closing the private financing round before mid-November allows OpenAI to bypass immediate scrutiny from investors who may use Anthropic's IPO registration statements to demand greater clarity from OpenAI Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar regarding revenue growth and financial outlook.

Market Headwinds and Infrastructure Demands

The capital scramble arrives amid an uncertain macroeconomic backdrop for artificial intelligence companies. Tech equity markets have experienced heightened volatility since September 2026, driven by rising bond yields, energy market swings, and growing scrutiny of debt-funded computing infrastructure. Companies tracked in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index shed $1.6 trillion in market capitalization in less than two weeks in October, as reported by Reuters.

Investor caution has been magnified by pressures on specialized cloud providers. CoreWeave experienced declines in the value of its equity and debt securities as investors examined the nearly $50 billion in borrowings the firm took on to fund data center expansions. Market hesitation has prompted several late-stage enterprises to postpone liquidity events. Blackstone-backed AirTrunk REIT, Nvidia-backed Firmus, and SoftBank-backed SB Energy have all paused or withdrawn listings. Nuclear energy provider Holtec likewise canceled a planned United States market listing in September 2026, citing a difficult environment caused by shifting data center power requirements.

For OpenAI, raising $30 billion provides necessary financial insulation. The incoming capital is designated to support substantial infrastructure commitments and reinforce its balance sheet against further delays to its 2027 public listing if IPO conditions remain muted.

Questions people ask

Why is OpenAI accelerating its $30 billion funding round?

OpenAI is expediting the round to secure capital commitments from institutional and sovereign wealth investors before rival Anthropic lists its shares in mid-November 2026, avoiding direct public comparisons with Anthropic's financial disclosures.

What valuation is OpenAI seeking in this round?

The round is being discussed at a pre-money valuation of more than $1.4 trillion, up from an $852 billion valuation set during a $122 billion funding round completed in March 2026.

What are OpenAI's latest stated revenue figures?

OpenAI reported reaching $50 billion in annualized revenue following the launch of GPT-6 Astra, and it told investors it projects reaching $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of 2026.

What is the accounting disagreement between OpenAI and Anthropic?

OpenAI reports revenue on a net basis after subtracting revenue splits paid to partners, and has alleged Anthropic inflates revenue by recording the full gross contract value of models sold through third-party cloud providers. Anthropic maintains that its revenue reporting complies with established accounting standards.
