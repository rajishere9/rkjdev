Google Privacy Policy Permits Connected Gmail Data in Targeted Ads

A quiet policy revision establishes that opting into Google's Personal Intelligence AI tools can expose email and photo summaries to ad targeting.

In short

  • A privacy policy update effective October 16, 2026, creates an exception to Google's ban on using Gmail, Drive, and Photos data for personalized ads.
  • The exception activates when users connect their accounts to Personal Intelligence features in Google Search and Gemini.
  • Summaries, excerpts, and inferences derived from connected emails are also used to train generative AI models and can be viewed by human reviewers.
  • The integration is opt-in and currently excludes users in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, and Nigeria.
  • Users can disconnect apps in their Google Account settings, though previously retained data remains in Search Services History until deleted manually.

Google has revised its core privacy rules to allow content from personal inboxes and photo libraries to inform targeted advertising. The update, which took effect on October 16, 2026, modifies a long-standing corporate firewall that barred the company from deploying sensitive user content in Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos for commercial ad profiling.

The shift was first documented by Android Authority, revealing that while Google does not scan or use personal repositories for advertising by default, an explicit exception now applies whenever users link those services to the company's AI-assisted search tools.

The October 16 Privacy Policy Revision

For years, Google maintained an unequivocal guarantee regarding user content stored in its productivity suites. Prior to the revision, the official Google Privacy Policy stated plainly: "We don't show you personalized ads based on your content from Drive, Gmail, or Photos."

Effective October 16, 2026, the company appended a critical conditional clause to that declaration. The updated policy document reads that Google will not show personalized ads based on content from Drive, Gmail, or Photos "unless you choose to make data from these services available to other Google services that support personalized ads." As reported by Notebookcheck, the policy specifically identifies Search as an example, confirming that Search may process accessible data to tailor both general content and advertisements.

Google explained the operational framework in the updated text: "You may choose to allow certain Google services, including services like Search or Gemini Apps, to access data from other Google services you use, such as Google Photos and Workspace apps like Gmail and Drive, in order to provide you with a more personalized experience, subject to the policies and settings of the accessing service."

How Personal Intelligence Feeds Ad Systems

The policy revision directly tethers user content to Personal Intelligence, an architecture that Google introduced in early 2026. According to The Hacker News, the feature allows Gemini and Google Search to analyze external repositories such as Gmail, Google Photos, and YouTube to provide context-aware responses rather than generic search outputs.

Under this workflow, an individual querying Search for dining options near an upcoming hotel booking can allow the system to inspect travel itineraries residing in Gmail. However, once those messages are surfaced to AI Mode or other Search tools, the information transitions out of the protected productivity silo.

In a statement reported by Ars Technica, a Google spokesperson sought to downplay direct surveillance of personal communications: "Connecting your apps is entirely opt-in. Ads will continue to be relevant to things like your query, the context of the response and your interests. Ads will not be based directly on your Gmail inbox or Photos library, and as always, sensitive information will not be used to tailor ads."

Despite the assertion that ads are not based directly on inboxes, the underlying mechanisms rely on secondary signals. According to technical documentation analyzed by Notebookcheck, Google uses summaries, excerpts, and algorithmic inferences derived from connected inboxes and photo libraries to tailor commercial promotions. A Google spokesperson confirmed to Search Engine Land that the company has already initiated active ad testing within AI Mode for users who connected their applications through Personal Intelligence.

Model Training and Human Review

The integration of personal accounts into Search services carries data implications that extend beyond commercial marketing. As reported by Notebookcheck, Google's support documentation confirms that connecting apps also permits the company to use generated summaries and inferences to train generative AI models.

A subset of that data is reviewed by human evaluators, including trained service providers. For shorter or highly relevant emails, the system summary used for review and model optimization can be identical to the full text of the original email. Google's official documentation advises users against connecting accounts if they contain confidential or personal information they do not want processed by human reviewers or absorbed into model datasets.

Google maintains that core protections remain functional. The company does not share personally identifiable details, such as full names or email addresses, directly with external advertisers without explicit user consent. Furthermore, sensitive personal categories—including racial identity, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, and medical health conditions—remain formally excluded from ad targeting parameters.

Geographic Availability and Industry Context

The Personal Intelligence rollout and its accompanying data exceptions are subject to strict regional segmentation. According to The Hacker News, the connected feature is distributed in beta to eligible users aged 18 and older globally, but it is explicitly excluded from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, and Nigeria.

Earlier in October 2026, German data protection authorities issued formal warnings regarding commercial profiling drawn from conversational chatbot histories.

Google is not alone in exploring conversational monetization. In January 2026, OpenAI introduced advertisements for adult users in the United States across free and ChatGPT Go subscription tiers, subsequently expanding ad placement to 89 countries. OpenAI has maintained that conversational records are not sold to third parties and that sponsored placements do not alter the analytical answers produced by its engine.

In parallel with the search revisions, Google modified its workspace integrations across its chat interfaces. As noted by Notebookcheck, Gemini Notebook launched on October 23 as an active connected app enabled by default worldwide, while remaining turned off by default for accounts registered within the European Economic Area.

Account Controls and Data Retention Limits

Users who prefer to keep their emails and photo archives completely isolated from ad systems must manage account-level toggles manually. Android Authority published specific procedural steps to revoke Search access while maintaining general Google services:

  1. Sign into the primary Google Account and select Data & privacy.
  2. Navigate through Personalization & activity to access Personalization settings.
  3. Open Manage Personalized Recommendations in Search Services.
  4. Locate Connected Content apps under the Additional Personal Intelligence menu.
  5. Toggle off Google Workspace and Google Photos.

Alternatively, users can deactivate Personalized Recommendations altogether through the Search Services Personalization dashboard at google.com/search-personalization. Ad preferences can be inspected separately inside My Ad Center.

Disconnecting services does not automatically scrub previously collected material. As Decrypt highlighted, information that has already been ingested into a user's Search Services History can remain stored on Google's servers even after the source app is detached or the original email is deleted. Complete removal requires users to manually clear records through activity.google.com.

Questions people ask

Does Google automatically scan all Gmail messages to show targeted ads?

No. The policy update specifies that Gmail, Drive, and Photos data is not used for personalized ads by default. The exception only applies if an account holder explicitly opts into connecting those applications to services like Google Search via Personal Intelligence.

Do advertisers receive direct access to emails or photos?

Google states that it does not provide raw email or photo files to advertisers, nor does it share personally identifying details such as names or email addresses without explicit permission. Ads are informed by algorithmic summaries and inferences within Google's own systems.

Why is this feature unavailable in Europe and the United Kingdom?

Personal Intelligence is currently restricted to users outside the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and Nigeria.

Does disconnecting Gmail from Search delete my existing history?

No. Disconnecting Workspace apps stops future data sharing, but previously compiled summaries and interaction records can remain stored in Search Services History until a user manually purges them at activity.google.com.
