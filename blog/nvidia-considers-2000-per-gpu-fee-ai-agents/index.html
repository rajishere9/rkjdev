Nvidia Floats $2,000 Annual Per-GPU Fee for AI Factory Agent Software

Speaking at GTC Berlin, CEO Jensen Huang outlined a potential recurring subscription model for enterprise systems orchestrating autonomous AI agents.

In short

  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang indicated software running AI agents in 'AI factories' could cost approximately $2,000 per GPU annually.
  • The floated price point is less than half the $4,500 annual subscription Nvidia currently charges per GPU for its AI Enterprise platform.
  • Nvidia has not officially announced a price cut or a distinct product SKU, leaving rollout timing and scope undefined.
  • The pricing discussion followed Nvidia's October 21 introduction of the OpenShell Reference Architecture and Nvidia Sentry security tooling.

Nvidia is weighing a recurring software licensing model that would charge enterprise customers roughly $2,000 per GPU each year to run autonomous AI agents in high-density data centers. Speaking at the GTC Berlin event on October 22, 2026, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang singled out software licensing as a central growth driver for the company's infrastructure portfolio.

According to reporting by Tom's Hardware, Huang stated that running Nvidia's full agent software suite across enterprise infrastructure could center on an annual per-chip licensing cost. "Software licensing will become increasingly a major part of our company’s business," Huang said during the event. "The way that we license will be…for example, it’s about $2,000 per GPU per year to run all the software that Nvidia provides that runs agents on these AI factories."

Unpacking the $2,000 Software Licensing Model

Huang's comments, initially reported by The Register and highlighted by TweakTown, point toward a strategy designed to monetize recurring workloads rather than relying solely on one-off hardware sales. Nvidia currently brings in the vast majority of its revenue by selling data center accelerators.

A hypothetical scenario outlined by FourWeekMBA and cited by Tom's Hardware illustrates the potential scale: licensing 5 million GPUs at an annual rate of $2,000 would produce $10 billion in annual recurring revenue, totaling $50 billion across a five-year period if clients maintained their subscriptions. The sources emphasize that these numbers represent an illustrative calculation rather than an official company forecast. Nvidia has not shared official projections for how many GPUs might qualify for such a software license or whether $2,000 represents a definitive rate.

Comparison With Nvidia AI Enterprise

The $2,000 figure stands in sharp contrast to Nvidia's existing enterprise software pricing. According to Notebookcheck, the company currently sells its Nvidia AI Enterprise platform for $4,500 per GPU for a one-year subscription with support, more than double the rate Huang mentioned in Berlin.

As noted by TweakTown, the $2,000 price point does not match any existing commercial product in Nvidia's current licensing guide. Nvidia has not published a formal price cut for its existing enterprise suite, nor has it clarified whether the $2,000 estimate refers to an agent-specific bundle, an unannounced subscription tier, or an overall restructuring of AI Enterprise.

Supporting Hardware and the OpenShell Architecture

The pricing remarks followed product announcements made earlier in the week. On October 21, 2026, Nvidia introduced the OpenShell Reference Architecture, an environment engineered to host autonomous AI agents securely and regulate access to system tools and corporate datasets, as reported by ARY News.

OpenShell functions as a runtime sandbox that isolates agent processes, ensuring an agent cannot access data outside the explicit requirements of a task. The reference architecture relies on Nvidia's Vera Rubin computing infrastructure, Vera CPUs, and BlueField-4 data processing units (DPUs). These DPUs run Nvidia Sentry, an independent monitoring program capable of quarantining agents that cross defined security limits. Nvidia previously confirmed in June that OpenShell will also support Windows environments.

Cost Pressures and Enterprise Scrutiny

AI agents can use tools, access data, and carry out tasks with limited human intervention. However, businesses deploying these systems at scale need software to manage their workloads and prevent agents from accessing resources they shouldn't.

While software provides the governance needed for these agents, adding a recurring $2,000 annual charge per processor could add substantial operational overhead. As reported by TweakTown, data center operators and enterprise customers are already facing heavy capital expenditures for accelerator hardware, which may prompt close review of any mandatory or complementary recurring software fees Nvidia brings to market.
