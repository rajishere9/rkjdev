New Mexico Court Orders Meta to Pay $25 Billion in Privacy Ruling

State District Court Judge Francis Mathew reduced an initial $219.5 billion calculation to $25 billion, directing the proceeds to public schools.

In short

  • New Mexico District Court Judge Francis Mathew ordered Meta to pay a $25 billion civil penalty for misleading consumers on data privacy.
  • A Santa Fe jury found Meta committed more than 43 million violations of the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act based on 26 deceptive statements.
  • Unpaid penalties will accrue interest at an annual rate of 15 percent, with all proceeds allocated to the state public school system.
  • Meta plans to appeal the decision, with chief legal officer C.J. Mahoney calling the enforcement action a tax on speech.

On October 21, 2026, a New Mexico state court ordered Meta Platforms to pay $25 billion in civil penalties for misleading consumers about how it handles user data and content moderation. The ruling, handed down by District Court Judge Francis Mathew in Santa Fe, follows a September jury verdict finding that the social media giant violated state consumer protection laws tens of millions of times. The judgment represents one of the largest consumer privacy penalties ever issued against a technology corporation, according to The Washington Post.

The judgment follows a series of regulatory pressures on the tech company. While federal and multistate coalitions previously settled related claims, New Mexico pursued its independent action to trial.

The Anatomy of the $25 Billion Judgment

Former New Mexico Attorney General Hector Balderas originally filed the lawsuit in 2021 in the wake of the Cambridge Analytica data harvesting revelations, while current Attorney General Raúl Torrez brought the case through trial. As reported by Engadget, the litigation did not target the 2018 harvesting of data by Cambridge Analytica directly. Instead, the complaint focused on Facebook's public statements regarding user data protections, platform privacy controls, and content moderation.

In his ruling, Judge Mathew wrote that Facebook's post-scandal internal audit was "willfully ineffective." According to court findings detailed by CNBC, internal evaluations stalled due to executive pressure to hold down costs, an absence of adequate investigative tools, insufficient enforcement resources, and an active desire to avoid setting internal precedents for future inquiries. Mathew concluded that the company's repeated misstatements were both intentional and systematic.

Calculating 43 Million Statutory Violations

On September 25, 2026, a Santa Fe jury found that 26 out of 29 statements made by the company and its executives were misleading under the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act. Under state law, civil penalties can reach up to $5,000 per violation. Because each resident or user who received the deceptive communications was counted as an individual violation, the jury tallied more than 43 million separate infractions.

CBC reported that a penalty of more than $219 billion was warranted. During an October 1 hearing, New Mexico counsel Randi McGinn asked the court to award between $35 billion and $40 billion, arguing that the court needed to address Meta in the financial terms of stock valuation. Meta requested that Mathew cap the penalty at $3.45 billion, claiming that the state failed to prove that any consumer had actually been misled and warning that large fines would deter commerce in New Mexico.

Judge Mathew established the final fine at $25 billion. Under the order, the penalty will accrue interest at an annual rate of 15 percent until paid in full. The state will allocate the recovered funds to New Mexico's public school system.

Bypassing Global Settlements to Reach Trial

New Mexico secured the massive judgment because it opted out of broader national settlements. In August 2026, Meta entered into an overarching settlement with a coalition of state attorneys general covering youth mental health and child safety claims, which included roughly $459 million earmarked for Cambridge Analytica data harvesting disputes. While that multistate resolution totaled between $16.7 billion and $18 billion nationwide, New Mexico declined to participate, preserving its right to try the consumer fraud claims separately.

This marks the state's second major trial against Meta in 2026. In March, a Santa Fe jury found Meta liable for $375 million in penalties under the Unfair Practices Act regarding child safety on its platforms. In a subsequent phase of that proceedings, a state judge ordered the company to pay $567 million into a teen mental health abatement fund after declaring the company's platform design a public nuisance.

Meta confirmed it will appeal the $25 billion ruling. Meta Chief Legal Officer C.J. Mahoney argued that the state's enforcement strategy makes conducting business in New Mexico untenable, calling the penalty a tax on speech aimed at imposing state-level rules on online discourse.

Questions people ask

Why was Meta fined $25 billion in New Mexico?

A New Mexico state court found Meta liable for deceptive business practices after a jury determined that the company issued 26 misleading statements regarding user data privacy, platform controls, and content moderation.

Where will the $25 billion penalty go?

State law and court orders direct all recovered funds from the civil penalty to New Mexico's public school system.

What interest rate applies to the penalty?

According to the ruling by Judge Francis Mathew, the unpaid civil penalty will accrue interest at an annual rate of 15 percent until satisfied in full.

How did Meta respond to the court order?

Meta chief legal officer C.J. Mahoney stated that the company is confident it will prevail on appeal, describing the ruling as a tax on speech.
