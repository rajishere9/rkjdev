Character.AI Files for Chapter 11 as Filings Reveal Heavy Financial Burn

Court documents reveal millions in monthly compute losses, prompting a court-supervised sale where Disney serves as the lead bidder.

In short

  • Character.AI initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy process in Delaware, estimating both assets and liabilities between $50 million and $100 million.
  • Court affidavits show the company spent $56.1 million on AI compute alone between January and August 2026 while losing $8 million to $9 million each month.
  • Disney Worldwide Services emerged as the stalking horse bidder, offering $20 million for undisclosed assets and a non-exclusive perpetual intellectual property license.
  • The platform remains operational, but management confirmed it will be wound down if the court-supervised sale does not secure a viable buyer by late November or early December 2026.

Character.AI has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in Delaware to facilitate a court-supervised sale of the company. The voluntary filing, announced in an official company update, marks a sharp turning point for one of generative AI's earliest breakout consumer platforms. Court records detail years of steep operating losses, heavy server expenditures, and compounding legal headwinds that outpaced the startup's revenue growth.

Under the proposed restructuring, Character.AI has lined up Disney Worldwide Services as an opening bidder. If no competing offer emerges to sustain operations, company leadership warned that the interactive chatbot platform will shut down.

Unpacking Character.AI's Severe Cash Burn and Compute Costs

While Character.AI attracted millions of dedicated users, court records filed this week reveal that its underlying business model remained deeply unsustainable. In an affidavit submitted to the bankruptcy court, Character.AI Chief Executive Officer Colin Adams stated that the platform's technology is expensive to run and that the business has struggled with profitability throughout its existence, as reported by 404 Media.

Financial disclosures show the startup spent $13.8 million per month on AI compute during 2024, followed by $10 million per month in 2025. Between January and August 2026, the company spent another $56.1 million purely on compute capacity, alongside $19.7 million on employee compensation and benefits. Despite cuts, Character.AI was still burning between $8 million and $9 million each month as of August 2026.

Revenues never matched those operational expenses. According to court filings cited by 404 Media, monthly subscription revenue peaked at $2.8 million and occasionally dipped below $1 million. Total income from all streams, including advertising, reached a high of only $5.3 million per month. The gap forced management to drastically scale back operations.

To curb cash burn, Character.AI shifted away from training its own proprietary foundation models, instead post-training open-weight architectures and moving inference workloads to cheaper GPU capacity. The startup also laid off employees, instituted usage boundaries, launched in-app ads in mid-2025, and introduced in-app purchases in early 2026. Filings noted that multiple workers resigned during this period after questioning the company's long-term commercial viability.

Legal Liabilities and the Lead Bid From Disney

Operational expenses were compounded by mounting regulatory and legal challenges. As reported by Business Insider, the company faced intense scrutiny over its impact on minors, leading to lawsuits from families alleging the chatbots contributed to teenage mental health crises and instances of self-harm. Earlier in 2026, Character.AI and Google reached an agreement in principle to settle several of those claims.

Regulators also targeted the service. Texas opened an investigation into child privacy and safety compliance, and Pennsylvania sued the startup over automated bots impersonating medical professionals. The pressures prompted Character.AI to ban users under 18 from holding open-ended conversations on its site.

According to court documents outlined by The Verge, Character.AI listed estimated liabilities between $50 million and $100 million alongside assets in the same range. Disney has stepped in as the stalking horse bidder, agreeing to pay $20 million for select assets and a non-exclusive, perpetual technology license. Disney previously clashed with Character.AI, issuing a cease-and-desist letter over unauthorized bots based on copyrighted characters. However, Disney recently hired former Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand as its chief technology officer, and former startup personnel now at Disney will provide transition support.

Platform Status and the Path Toward December

Founded in late 2021 by former Google researchers Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, Character.AI raised over $193 million from venture firms such as Andreessen Horowitz, Lightspeed, SV Angel, and Greycroft, according to TechCrunch. In August 2024, Google completed a $2.7 billion licensing agreement with Character.AI that saw Shazeer and De Freitas return to Google.

Character.AI stated in its official blog that platform services, paid subscriptions, and billing remain operational for now. Data privacy policies stay intact, and users retain the ability to export their conversation histories or delete their profiles.

Management expects the court-supervised sale to wrap up by late November or early December 2026. If another bidder does not come forward with a proposal to fund and sustain the consumer platform beyond Disney's licensing offer, Character.AI confirmed it will formally wind down operations.

Questions people ask

Will the Character.AI platform continue running?

Yes, for now. Character.AI stated that its website, mobile app, and existing paid subscriptions will remain active while the court-supervised sale process proceeds through late November or early December 2026. However, if no buyer steps forward to fund ongoing platform operations, the service will be wound down.

What is Disney purchasing from Character.AI?

Disney Worldwide Services is serving as the opening stalking horse bidder, agreeing to pay $20 million for certain undisclosed assets and a non-exclusive, perpetual license to Character.AI technology. The deal also includes transition support from former startup employees hired by Disney.

Can users delete or export their chat data?

Yes. Character.AI confirmed that data privacy policies remain unchanged during the Chapter 11 process, and users can export their chat data or delete their accounts directly through the app or website.
