Chery's AiMOGA Unveils Mobo Humanoid Companion Robot

AiMOGA Robotics, the robotics unit of Chinese automaker Chery Group, is expanding beyond commercial deployments with a 22-DoF companion robot powered by its MoAgent AI.

In short

  • AiMOGA Robotics announced global pre-orders for Mobo, a consumer humanoid companion robot, at the 2026 Chery International User Summit in Wuhu, China.
  • The humanoid features a 22-degree-of-freedom motion system, dynamic obstacle avoidance, and AiMOGA's proprietary MoAgent system for multi-step task planning.
  • AiMOGA leverages automotive technologies from parent company Chery Group, including its CheryMind model, sensor perception systems, and manufacturing supply chains.
  • The company has not yet released pricing, deposit requirements, or delivery timelines for Mobo.
  • AiMOGA reports having already deployed more than 3,000 commercial robotic units across 60 countries and regions.

AiMOGA Robotics, the robotics division of Chinese automaker Chery Group, is moving into the consumer market with the introduction of Mobo, its first humanoid companion robot designed for domestic settings. According to Markets Insider, Mobo's global debut and pre-orders are scheduled for October 21 during the 2026 Chery International User Summit held October 19–23 in Wuhu, China, which GlobeNewswire reported without specifying the October 21 debut date.

Prior to this launch, Mobo appeared at the World Robot Conference and the World Manufacturing Convention. The formal release at the Wuhu summit marks Chery's initial push to apply its automotive manufacturing, intelligent driving, and supply chain resources directly to domestic embodied artificial intelligence.

Goal-Driven Assistance and Household Navigation

Mobo is designed around household companionship, interaction, and educational support across multiple generations. According to GlobeNewswire, the robot runs on AiMOGA's proprietary MoAgent system, an architecture designed to parse user requests, break complex instructions down into actionable steps, and plan longer-term tasks rather than only responding to single conversational prompts.

For physical mobility and interaction, Mobo is equipped with a full-body motion system providing 22 degrees of freedom. AiMOGA states this allows the humanoid to perform expressive physical gestures, navigate living spaces stably, and perform activities such as dance routines.

Mobo's perception stack combines spatial perception with multimodal interaction. Using integrated computer vision, voice recognition, and contextual conversational awareness, the robot can identify household objects, discuss nearby items, and follow dynamic obstacle avoidance paths as it moves through indoor rooms, as reported by GlobeNewswire. Long-term memory capabilities allow it to track family members' individual routines, retain conversational context over time, and issue schedule reminders. The manufacturer noted that privacy protections were incorporated into the system's architecture from the outset.

Adapting Automotive Manufacturing to Embodied AI

AiMOGA's path into humanoid robotics relies heavily on the engineering and supply infrastructure of its parent company, Chery Group. Drawing on Chery's global R&D system, AiMOGA has established 31 in-house research and development laboratories focusing on intelligent perception, motion control, and vehicle-robot synergy, according to Business Insider.

Under this technical setup, Mobo adapts environmental perception systems and AI capabilities developed for intelligent vehicle cockpits and assisted driving. This includes Chery's domain-specific CheryMind large AI model, as detailed by GlobeNewswire. By drawing on Chery's expertise in supply chain management, manufacturing, and quality control, AiMOGA aims to support practical, scalable robotics deployment.

While Mobo represents AiMOGA's first dedicated consumer-facing humanoid, the company has already built a presence in professional service robotics. AiMOGA reports cumulative global deliveries exceeding 3,000 units across more than 60 countries and regions, covering over 100 operational use cases such as government service centers, automotive dealerships, healthcare facilities, and retail locations, according to The AI Insider.

Demonstrations at the Chery Global Technology Innovation Center during the summit will feature AiMOGA's wider commercial portfolio alongside Mobo. These systems include specialized traffic police patrol units, the Mornine retail and livestreaming robot, a robotic nurse designed for hospital guidance, and the Argos Pro quadruped robot, which will demonstrate autonomous following and cargo transport.

Market Positioning and Availability Gaps

Although AiMOGA will open global pre-orders on October 21, critical retail details remain unknown. As reported by The AI Insider, the company did not disclose the purchase price, required pre-order deposit amounts, regional delivery schedules, or formal shipment dates for Mobo.

Questions people ask

What is AiMOGA Mobo designed to do?

Mobo is a consumer-grade humanoid companion robot designed for domestic settings. It handles multi-step task planning, recognizes household objects, provides schedule reminders, navigates rooms with dynamic obstacle avoidance, and remembers family members' habits.

Who manufactures Mobo?

Mobo is developed by AiMOGA Robotics, the robotics division of Chinese automaker Chery Group and affiliated with its EXEED brand.

How much does Mobo cost and when does it ship?

AiMOGA has not publicly disclosed pricing, reservation deposit amounts, or initial shipment schedules alongside its pre-order announcement.

What AI model powers Mobo's planning and navigation?

Mobo uses AiMOGA's proprietary MoAgent system for task breakdown and spatial awareness, incorporating technologies derived from Chery's automotive R&D and its CheryMind AI model.
