OpenAI Launches Rosalind Academic Plan for U.S. University Labs

Eligible academic labs at U.S. universities can receive up to 30 complimentary Business Standard seats for 12 months to run life sciences research on GPT-Rosalind.

In short

  • OpenAI introduced the Rosalind Academic Plan on October 15, 2026, granting eligible U.S. university labs up to 30 free Business Standard seats for up to 12 months.
  • Access to GPT-Rosalind and its tool-orchestrating Rosalind Workbench requires a specialized trusted-access screening process.
  • Research data submitted under the academic plan is not used to train OpenAI models by default.
  • API access is excluded from the academic tier, and applicant labs must provide a designated institutional biosafety contact.

On October 15, 2026, OpenAI introduced the Rosalind Academic Plan, a sponsored initiative designed to supply university laboratories with access to specialized biological artificial intelligence tools. Under the program, eligible academic research labs at U.S. universities can receive up to 30 ChatGPT Business Standard seats at no cost for up to 12 months. The core offering pairs access to standard frontier systems with GPT-Rosalind, OpenAI's dedicated life sciences model, subject to institutional vetting and trusted-access approval. According to OpenAI's official announcement, the program seeks to lower financial barriers for researchers conducting foundational investigations across biology, diagnostics, and therapeutics.

OpenAI framed the program around foundational scientific discoveries, highlighting how curiosity-driven research on bacterial immune systems yielded CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing and decades of university work enabled mRNA vaccines. By sponsoring workspace licenses for up to a year, the initiative allows academic scientists to deploy dedicated biological reasoning models at no cost.

What the Rosalind Academic Plan Includes

The plan provides participating groups with a managed ChatGPT Business workspace containing up to 30 Business Standard seats priced at $0 per user per month for a 12-month period. According to the OpenAI Help Center guide, standard seats include access to ChatGPT and Codex, alongside commercially available frontier OpenAI models, subject to regular plan usage limits. The complimentary status does not lift standard rate or usage caps.

OpenAI stated that research data shared through the Rosalind Academic Plan is not used to train company models by default. However, participating workspaces operate under standard data retention rules, and the offer does not support Zero Data Retention. Workspace administrative authority is concentrated entirely in the verified principal investigator (PI) or lab lead, who serves as the sole workspace owner and cannot name secondary owners or administrators. Additionally, adding researchers to the workspace does not automatically unlock GPT-Rosalind; the PI must manually activate permissions for individual named lab members.

The plan carries concrete product exclusions. API access is not included, as reported by The Decoder, and the offer includes no API credits, as reported by Unite.AI. Thirty seats represents the maximum capacity permitted in an approved workspace. Labs requiring additional seats cannot extend the free tier by launching separate paid accounts, which do not inherit the GPT-Rosalind approval.

Dedicated Tools and Life Sciences Capabilities

OpenAI says GPT-Rosalind is built to handle complex scientific questions and reasoning across life sciences disciplines. Rather than operating purely as a text-generation chat interface, the model integrates with the Rosalind Workbench, an orchestrated workspace currently in research preview. As reported by Unite.AI, the Workbench features an Explore mode for general inquiries and a Research mode configured to coordinate domain tools for wet-lab assistance, genomics, and medicinal chemistry.

Target research areas cited by OpenAI include drug and protein design, disease detection, vaccines, genetic and RNA therapies, agriculture, animal health, ecology, and antimicrobial resistance. Early academic groups are already evaluating the platform inside active research workflows. At the University of California, San Francisco, the Zhao Lab applied GPT-Rosalind to gather drug property parameters across chemical databases, while researchers at Stanford University and the Arc Institute are examining the system for discovery and molecular design tasks.

To demonstrate the model's specialized scope, OpenAI published a prompt comparison evaluating a request for a 20-variant sequence matrix for a second-generation Nipah antiviral lipopeptide campaign. In OpenAI's example, GPT-Rosalind generated candidate comparison tables, modeling blueprints, and paired assay outlines with control mechanisms. By contrast, OpenAI's Mainline Astra system withheld implementation-level experimental blueprints and specific sequence variants, responding that such outputs would provide actionable assistance involving high-consequence pathogens. OpenAI noted that its published examples assess textual fulfillment of deliverables rather than scientific accuracy or experimental viability in a physical lab.

Eligibility Criteria and Safety Safeguards

Participation in the Rosalind Academic Plan is currently restricted to academic laboratories situated at U.S. universities. Independent researchers and commercial biotechnology firms are excluded. The application path depends on existing institutional agreements: PIs at universities lacking an OpenAI Enterprise contract must apply directly through the trusted-access process, whereas researchers at institutions holding an Enterprise contract must route requests through their institutional Enterprise process, according to aipolix.com.

To apply, lab leads must use an institutional .edu email account and verify their personal identity and institutional standing via Persona. The application asks for lab details, website addresses, active project descriptions, intended use cases, and the specific members requesting access. Every added member must possess a verified email address on the identical university domain as the lead investigator, precluding visiting fellows or external cross-university collaborators from sharing the workspace.

Applications must also provide the name and contact details of a trained biosafety official from the institution's Environmental Health and Safety, Research Safety, or biosecurity office. This individual must hold formal institutional authority over the proposed experimental work. After approval, OpenAI maintains ongoing compliance monitoring, reserving the right to follow up on high-sensitivity queries to confirm context, as detailed by Technobezz, or revoke access if safe research requirements are breached, as reported by Unite.AI.

Practical Implications for Academic Labs

Offering up to 30 Business Standard seats for 12 months provides a sponsored mechanism to deploy AI-driven molecular reasoning and workbench tooling without preliminary software licensing fees. However, because a payment card remains required during setup and standard usage caps persist, lab leads must treat the plan as a managed, regulated enterprise environment rather than an unconstrained compute grant.

API access is not included under this free tier. Instead, work must proceed inside the interactive ChatGPT interface, Codex, and the Rosalind Workbench.

Questions people ask

What is included in the Rosalind Academic Plan?

Eligible labs receive up to 30 ChatGPT Business Standard seats at no cost for 12 months, covering frontier OpenAI models, Codex, the Rosalind Workbench, and trusted-access access to GPT-Rosalind.

Is API access included in the free academic plan?

No, API access and API credits are not included in the Rosalind Academic Plan. GPT-Rosalind cannot be accessed via the API under this sponsored program.

Who is eligible to apply for the Rosalind Academic Plan?

The plan is currently open only to academic research laboratories at U.S. universities. Commercial organizations and unaffiliated independent researchers are not eligible.

Does OpenAI use academic plan research data to train its models?

OpenAI states that research data shared through the Rosalind Academic Plan is not used to train its models by default.
