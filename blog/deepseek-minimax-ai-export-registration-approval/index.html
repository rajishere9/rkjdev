DeepSeek and MiniMax Clear AI Export Registration for Global Rollout

Chinese AI developers DeepSeek and MiniMax cleared initial export registration under draft technology trade rules, preserving open weights and global licensing.

In short

  • DeepSeek and MiniMax completed export registration under China's proposed AI export control catalogue, according to state broadcaster CCTV affiliate Yuyuan Tantian on October 22, 2026.
  • The Chinese Ministry of Commerce stated that the vast majority of AI technologies will remain exportable under simplified licensing frameworks such as 'export upon filing.'
  • API access, SaaS interfaces, and public open-source weights remain unhindered, but local overseas deployments of restricted models require registration or compliance licensing.
  • DeepSeek open-sourced DeepInfer and deepseek-recipe on October 20, 2026, while MiniMax introduced a compliance review system for users in the US, EU, UK, and South Korea.

Two of China's most prominent artificial intelligence developers, DeepSeek and MiniMax, have completed initial AI export registration requirements under proposed trade controls, enabling them to proceed with open-source software releases and international commercial licensing. The regulatory clearance was first reported on the evening of October 22, 2026, by Yuyuan Tantian, a social media channel operated by state broadcaster CCTV, according to coverage by Pandaily and The News International.

The filings mark the first practical application of proposed revisions to China's export control apparatus. On October 16, 2026, the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Science and Technology released a draft revision of the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted From Export for public comment. The draft proposed export controls covering 23 AI-related technologies, including large models, autonomous agents, computing clusters, and information-processing systems, establishing specific thresholds on model parameters and computing volume.

How the AI Export Registration Framework Functions

The completion of registration by DeepSeek and MiniMax demonstrates how Beijing intends to enforce the proposed regulations without cutting off international software distribution. According to reporting from Analytics Insight, the Ministry of Commerce indicated that the vast majority of AI technologies will remain exportable under streamlined procedures.

Under the ministry's framework, technologies that have already entered the public domain through academic papers or open-source distribution fall outside the scope of restrictive export management. For emerging technologies covered by the catalogue, the regulatory body outlined three operational tiers:

First, open-sourcing AI code, building and running overseas data centers owned by Chinese firms, and conducting joint research using foreign infrastructure qualify for an automatic licensing procedure described by officials as close to "export upon filing."

Second, providing access to a restricted model via remote application programming interfaces (APIs), software-as-a-service (SaaS) products, mobile applications, web portals, or lightweight on-device deployments does not count as exporting large-model technology.

Third, deploying a restricted model locally on infrastructure outside China falls under export management. However, deploying models on an enterprise's own overseas cloud infrastructure remains eligible for the automatic licensing route, according to Pandaily.

Implementation Across DeepSeek and MiniMax

Both companies took operational steps to align with the regulatory baseline immediately following the policy announcement. According to Chinese tech publication Huxiu, DeepSeek open-sourced its large-scale inference framework DeepInfer alongside a companion toolkit titled deepseek-recipe on October 20, 2026.

MiniMax instituted a formal application mechanism to handle model access in regions previously excluded by its earlier model license. The new procedure maintains open model weights while requiring organizations in the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, and South Korea to pass a compliance review and secure a license before running local overseas deployments, as detailed by Pandaily.

Reuters reported via WHTC and AsiaOne that neither DeepSeek nor MiniMax immediately replied to requests for comment regarding their filings. In a subsequent post on Friday, October 23, 2026, Yuyuan Tantian stated that overseas internet users had called on other Chinese open-source large language model developers to adopt the same registration pathway.

Practical Implications for International Developers

For international software developers and enterprise architects, the registrations offer clarity on the availability of Chinese open-weight systems. Open weights, open code, and API access are expected to continue largely as before, easing concerns that proposed controls would cut off overseas access. As reported by VKTR.com, open-weight models have attracted interest from enterprise users seeking architectural flexibility, local fine-tuning on proprietary data, and reduced vendor dependence.

The regulatory process remains subject to refinement. The draft catalogue opened by China's commerce and science ministries on October 16, 2026, remains under public review through November 15, 2026. Final provisions implemented after the consultation window could adjust technical thresholds or specific administrative burdens before taking permanent legal effect.

Questions people ask

What approvals did DeepSeek and MiniMax receive?

DeepSeek and MiniMax completed pre-export registration under China's proposed AI export rules, clearing their models for open-source releases, overseas licensing, and international deployment.

What technologies are covered under China's draft export catalogue?

The draft catalogue proposed on October 16, 2026, includes 23 AI-related technologies covering large models, AI agents, computing clusters, and information processing systems, with thresholds based on parameters and computing capacity.

Do developers using DeepSeek or MiniMax via API need export licenses?

No. The Ministry of Commerce clarified that accessing models through APIs, SaaS applications, web portals, or lightweight on-device deployments does not count as exporting large-model technology.

When does the public consultation for the draft export catalogue end?

The public comment period opened by the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Science and Technology runs through November 15, 2026.
