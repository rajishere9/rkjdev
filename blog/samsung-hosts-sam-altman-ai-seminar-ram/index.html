Samsung Hosts Sam Altman for Executive AI Seminar Amid Memory Crunch

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman met with 50 Samsung executives in South Korea to discuss AI strategy, data center infrastructure, and memory supply following controversial comments on DRAM allocation.

In short

  • OpenAI CEO Sam Altman met with approximately 50 senior Samsung executives on October 31, 2026, in Yongin, South Korea, to discuss corporate AI strategy.
  • The discussions build on an existing agreement to supply memory semiconductors for OpenAI's Stargate data center initiative.
  • The meeting followed Altman's public remarks calling the presence of DRAM in consumer personal computers inefficient amid ongoing global memory constraints.
  • Samsung executives participated in hands-on generative AI training immediately prior to the session as part of a group-wide AI transformation.

On October 31, 2026, Samsung hosted OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman for a high-level seminar at the Samsung Human Resources Development Center in Yongin, South Korea. The gathering brought together roughly 50 presidents and senior executives from Samsung affiliates spanning electronics, financial services, and biotechnology. Held under the theme "The Next Decade Shaped by AI: Corporate Choices," the session focused on operational decision-making, emerging commercial opportunities, and leadership requirements in an AI-driven economy.

The seminar arrived at a critical juncture for both organizations. As reported by Samsung Newsroom Korea, the meeting was designed to improve executive understanding of AI capabilities while aligning with Samsung's broader corporate transformation goals. The gathering also took place amid tightening hardware constraints across the artificial intelligence sector, with memory availability remaining a central bottleneck for cloud compute operators.

Inside the Yongin Executive AI Seminar

The meeting in Yongin was conducted by Song Yong-ho, Vice President and Head of the AX/PI Center at Samsung Electronics, according to Chosun.com. Key attendees included Jun Young-hyun, Vice Chairman of Samsung Electronics and head of the Device Solutions semiconductor division; Roh Tae-moon, President and head of the Device eXperience finished products division; and Park Hark-kyu, President and Head of Business Support. Leadership from other group arms was also present, including Samsung SDI CEO Choi Joo-sun and Samsung Electro-Mechanics CEO Chang Duck-hyun.

During the discussion, Altman cautioned executives that basic adoption of artificial intelligence would no longer provide a lasting market defense. "In the future, merely using AI will not guarantee sustainable competitive advantage," Altman said, according to Chosun.com. "Competitive edge depends on how AI is leveraged to move faster, create better products, and outpace rivals."

Altman also presented a physical gift to Song and attending executives: a "Codex Micro Keyboard" intended to streamline work with OpenAI's coding assistant Codex. Altman explained that the miniature device reflected a shift toward interacting with computers through spoken instruction and artificial intelligence, diminishing the necessity of a full keyboard.

Directly preceding the seminar, Samsung's affiliate heads completed a practical generative AI training session. The workshop was the second intensive program of its type for group leadership, following an initial round conducted in June 2026. According to The Korea Herald, executives practiced aggregating decentralized corporate information into personalized knowledge repositories and using AI analysis directly in commercial decision workflows.

The DRAM Crunch and Altman's Allocation Argument

The timing of Altman's visit to South Korea drew industry attention because of comments he made two days prior. In an appearance on Stripe co-founder John Collison's Cheeky Pint podcast released on October 29, 2026, Altman criticized the way global dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) is distributed across the market, as detailed by The Next Web.

Addressing the trend of running open-source models locally on personal machines, Altman argued that decentralized hardware use wastes scarce production output. "From a global allocation perspective, actually, it’s quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer and being used 5% of the time," Altman stated on the podcast. He added, "You want maximum utilization in the cloud if you’re trying to deal with this very supply constrained environment."

Altman noted that memory manufacturers were positioned to generate massive profits—stating they were "each going to make like $100 billion of free cash flow in a year"—but observed that past cyclical downturns and near-bankruptcies from overbuilding fabrication facilities have kept memory suppliers disciplined with their capital expenditures. The argument prompted pushback from advocates of consumer-level computing, as noted by Tom's Hardware, because OpenAI relies fundamentally on centralized data center capacity.

The global memory market remains severely constrained. Competitor SK Hynix stated in late October 2026 that memory shortages are anticipated to persist through 2030, while Apple chief executive John Ternus has labeled the supply situation a crisis. Samsung reported record quarterly earnings on the back of rising AI memory demand in the final week of October.

Expanding Infrastructure Ties and the Stargate Project

Altman's arrival underscores an expanding web of commercial relationships between Samsung and OpenAI. In October 2025, Samsung Electronics signed a letter of intent with OpenAI to cooperate on global AI infrastructure, focused primarily on providing memory semiconductors for OpenAI's large-scale Stargate data center development.

The framework encompasses multiple corporate divisions beyond standard memory sales. IT service unit Samsung SDS agreed to collaborate with OpenAI on enterprise AI offerings and data center deployment. Simultaneously, engineering and construction affiliates Samsung C&T and Samsung Heavy Industries initiated joint research with OpenAI on floating offshore data centers.

Executive contact between the firms has intensified across 2026. In June, OpenAI announced that Samsung Electronics would deploy ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex to staff across South Korea and within its global Device eXperience division. In July 2026, Samsung Electronics Executive Chairman Lee Jae-yong visited OpenAI's headquarters in San Francisco. Altman joins other American technology executives visiting South Korea to solidify supply chains; Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and AMD CEO Lisa Su have each completed two trips to the country since 2025.

Samsung's Internal AI Transformation Strategy

For Samsung, hosting OpenAI leadership aligns with a structured internal restructuring known as "AI Transformation" (AX), launched in June 2026 across research and development, manufacturing, sales, and corporate administrative units. Each affiliate has established dedicated internal teams tasked with overseeing data governance and orchestrating machine learning deployments.

According to Samsung's official disclosure, the group has completed AI training for approximately 2,300 corporate executives since June. The program is expanding to the general workforce, with the company aiming to provide generative AI training to all employees by February 2027.

The internal focus comes as Samsung navigates market volatility surrounding the semiconductor cycle. While Samsung skipped anniversary events for Executive Chairman Lee Jae-yong's fourth year in office and the company's 57th founding anniversary on November 1, division heads struck a cautious tone in messages released on October 30. Jun Young-hyun warned that "no one can guarantee how long the market boom will last," urging staff to prepare for structural shifts rather than relying solely on AI-fueled demand. Roh Tae-moon echoed the need to reinforce fundamental product engineering and elevate operational efficiency through AX integration.
