Microsoft Cancels UK Data Centre Lease Over Power Grid Delays

Microsoft terminates its lease at Nscale's planned Essex site after warnings that grid connections could face delays into the 2030s.

In short

  • Microsoft pulled out of a planned lease for Nscale's 50-megawatt artificial intelligence data centre site in Loughton, Essex.
  • UK Power Networks warned that national transmission capacity could delay adequate electricity delivery until the 2030s.
  • The facility was designed to house 23,040 Nvidia GB300 GPUs to support Microsoft Azure cloud computing workloads.
  • Nscale stated its long-term relationship with Microsoft remains intact and that it is actively exploring alternative power sources.

Microsoft has terminated its agreement to lease capacity at a planned artificial intelligence facility in Loughton, Essex, following severe transmission bottlenecks that could leave the project without sufficient electricity until the 2030s.

The site, developed by British infrastructure provider Nscale Global Holdings, was designed as an anchor location for Microsoft Azure cloud workloads. The tech giant's withdrawal deprives the Essex project of its only publicly announced customer and underscores the structural difficulties facing power-hungry compute facilities across the United Kingdom, as detailed by The Register.

Grid Delays Push Loughton Power to the 2030s

When unveiled in September 2025 as part of a £22 billion UK investment initiative, the Loughton development was promoted by political leaders as the nation's largest sovereign AI computing hub. Nscale had pledged £2 billion toward the facility, intending to deploy 23,040 Nvidia GB300 processors across an initial 50-megawatt footprint, with structural allowances to expand to 90 megawatts, according to BBC News.

Initial timetables slated the facility to go live by late 2026 or early 2027. However, regional distribution operator UK Power Networks warned that required national transmission upgrades would not be ready in time. Media reports confirmed local network officials cautioned that adequate power capacity might not reach the site until the early to mid-2030s.

UK Power Networks stated that while data centres can often plug into regional distribution systems, they are frequently forced to wait for broad reinforcements on the national transmission grid, which the local network does not operate, as reported by Morningstar.

Impact on Nscale and Upcoming IPO

Despite the cancellation, Nscale affirmed that it intends to continue developing the Essex site. "We remain committed to both the Loughton project and the U.K. and we are exploring alternative power sources for the site, in addition to other expansion sites in the U.K.," a company spokesperson said, adding that the London-wide grid issue has no bearing on its other operational contracts. A Microsoft spokesperson emphasized that the exit applies to a single facility and confirmed the company remains committed to its broader partnership with Nscale, according to Mint.

The cancellation arrives at a sensitive moment for Nscale, which filed a registration statement with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission ahead of a planned initial public offering on the New York Stock Exchange. The startup, which raised $3.36 billion in pre-IPO convertible financing in September 2026 and counts Nvidia, Dell Technologies, and Nokia among its backers, has targeted an IPO valuation of up to $35 billion.

Regulatory filings reveal that Microsoft and Anthropic together account for roughly 85% of Nscale's contracted revenue, with its single largest customer contributing 73% in fiscal year 2025, as reported by The Motley Fool.

Infrastructure Pressures Across European Compute Hubs

Securing sufficient baseload electricity has become the primary operational barrier for high-density compute facilities. British energy regulator Ofgem proposed charging developers upfront application fees after connection queues ballooned from 41 gigawatts to 125 gigawatts within a year, with computing projects making up at least 80 gigawatts of that total, according to Investing.com.

Faced with transmission delays, several British operators have turned to on-site gas-fired generation, while others face pushback over noise and municipal impacts. Nscale has faced operational constraints elsewhere as well; its flagship data facility in Norway is running six months behind schedule at 6.4 megawatts of its intended 30-megawatt capacity. Earlier in 2026, OpenAI backed out of a separate UK partnership with Nscale and Nvidia, pointing to local electricity expenses and regulatory uncertainties, as noted by TechRadar.

While Microsoft continues data centre buildouts elsewhere, the Loughton pullout demonstrates that capital and processor reservations cannot bypass physical transmission ceilings.

Questions people ask

Why did Microsoft cancel the Loughton data centre lease?

Microsoft terminated the lease because the facility faces extensive power grid connection delays, with local transmission capacity not expected to supply the site until the early 2030s.

What hardware was planned for the Loughton site?

Nscale planned to deploy 23,040 Nvidia GB300 chips inside the 50-megawatt facility to run Microsoft Azure cloud computing workloads.

Does the cancellation affect Microsoft's wider partnership with Nscale?

Both Microsoft and Nscale stated that the cancellation applies solely to the single Loughton site and does not affect their broader commercial partnership or other operational contracts.
