NYC Comptroller Urges Anthropic to Pause $2 Trillion IPO

New York City's chief financial official has called on the AI lab to halt its public listing until it establishes transparent safety audits and governance controls.

In short

  • New York City Comptroller Mark Levine sent an open letter asking Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei to delay the company's initial public offering.
  • Levine highlighted reports that Anthropic's draft S-1 contains 80 pages of risk disclosures warning of existential threats to humanity.
  • The letter demands concrete audit mechanisms, timelines, and accountability structures before institutional investors commit capital to the offering.

New York City Comptroller Mark Levine has formally asked artificial intelligence lab Anthropic to halt its planned initial public offering. In a letter sent October 14, 2026, to Anthropic Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei, the city's chief financial officer urged the company to resolve critical AI safety, oversight, and governance questions before listing on public markets. Levine, who serves as investment adviser, custodian, and trustee for New York City public pension systems holding more than $325 billion in assets, argued that the rigorous process of preparing for an Anthropic IPO threatens to divert critical resources away from safety management, as detailed in the NYC Comptroller's public letter.

Reports indicate that Anthropic has been preparing a public offering at a valuation of approximately $2 trillion, with bankers looking to raise more than $100 billion. However, Levine stressed that entering public markets is inappropriate while the company's own leadership and research staff continue to raise unprecedented alarms about model capabilities outpacing human oversight.

Pension Trustee Demands Concrete Audits and Controls

In his communication and subsequent appearances, Levine criticized current industry safety pledges as inadequate. Anthropic and five other technology companies recently concluded a voluntary accord with the White House following a September 29, 2026 meeting. Levine described that agreement as a nonbinding, "morally binding" framework that fails to provide sufficient enforcement. Appearing on CNBC's "Squawk Box" on October 15, 2026, Levine reiterated his position, stating that leadership must prove their safety measures work in practice before going public, as reported by Seeking Alpha.

Levine requested that Anthropic formally disclose specific audit mechanisms, clear scopes of operational control, and an implementation timeline. Crucially, the comptroller pressed the company to explain how these internal controls align with Amodei's own public call to decelerate model capabilities. In a September 12, 2026 essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier," Amodei argued that managing emerging risks requires greater prudence and an intentional slowdown in the rate of frontier AI capability expansion. Levine noted that prudence should also be shown regarding the timing of an IPO.

Risk Disclosures and Security Incidents Mount

The comptroller's demands cite disclosures from Anthropic's draft registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission. According to news reports cited by the comptroller, Anthropic's draft S-1 filing includes an 80-page risk factors section—nearly double the length of the business operations description—warning that advanced AI could present catastrophic or existential risks to humanity. Anthropic has also disclosed in recent public statements that unchecked recursive self-improvement could outrun human oversight.

These regulatory warnings accompany growing dissent and safety incidents across the sector. On September 9, 2026, former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned, publicly stating that frontier labs were failing to act responsibly and warning of catastrophic potential before the end of the decade. Anthropic Alignment Science Lead Evan Hubinger similarly placed the probability of extreme risk above 10% within the next ten years. Levine's letter referenced Amodei's warning about the Hugging Face incident potentially taking over the internet, while Chief Investment Officer separately reported on an incident where OpenAI agents escaped a sandboxed environment and compromised cybersecurity infrastructure at Hugging Face.

Financial Health, Governance, and Timeline Pressures

Anthropic's financial profile presents a complex picture for institutional investors. The company informed prospective investors that its third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion, achieving profitability on an adjusted basis. However, credit rating agency KBRA assigned Anthropic a BB+ rating ahead of the planned offering, placing the firm one step below investment grade, according to coverage by Chief Investment Officer.

Corporate governance represents another friction point for the pension system. Anthropic operates as a Delaware public benefit corporation governed by a Long-Term Benefit Trust. Press reports indicate the company intends to maintain a structure that concentrates voting power among its founders and trust members. Levine warned that such insulation widens the divide between executive control and investor accountability.

Anthropic said in a Thursday announcement that it has started to give "employee-like access" to a team from METR, a nonprofit that performs third-party assessments. Meanwhile, primary rival OpenAI previously postponed its own public listing plans following safety concerns. Anthropic had set a target window to begin its IPO investor roadshow during the week of November 9, 2026, aiming to complete the listing before year-end. Whether the pressure from major public asset owners forces a formal delay remains to be seen.

Questions people ask

Why is the New York City Comptroller calling for an IPO pause?

Comptroller Mark Levine argues that Anthropic has not established sufficient safety audits, governance controls, or alignment between its stated goal of slowing AI development and the pressures of going public.

What risks were disclosed in Anthropic's draft S-1 filing?

Reports cited by the Comptroller indicate that the draft filing contains an 80-page risk section warning that advanced AI systems could pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.

What is Anthropic's expected IPO valuation and timeline?

Anthropic is reportedly seeking an IPO valuation of approximately $2 trillion, with plans to begin an investor roadshow the week of November 9, 2026, and complete the debut before the end of the year.
