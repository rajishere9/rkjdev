Google Launches Gemini Agent to Automate Enterprise Workflows

Announced at Gemini at Work 2026, the universal agent can orchestrate long-running jobs, execute code across business systems, and operate under its own corporate identity.

Key takeaways

  • Google Cloud introduced the Gemini agent on October 8, 2026, designed to handle multi-step business objectives across devices and applications without requiring manual step-by-step instructions.
  • Persistent coworker configurations receive distinct organizational identities, complete with dedicated @agents.company.com email addresses, Google Drive storage, and calendar access.
  • The agent orchestrates workflows using both Google Gemini models and Anthropic Claude models, dynamically selecting models based on cost and workload requirements.
  • The software is available in private preview for select Gemini Enterprise and Google Workspace commercial customers, with broader availability planned without separate base fees.

Google Cloud introduced a universal workplace automation tool named the Gemini agent on October 8, 2026, shifting its enterprise artificial intelligence portfolio from passive conversational chatbots toward persistent software entities capable of carrying out multi-day assignments. Announced during the company's Gemini at Work 2026 keynote by Google Cloud Chief Executive Thomas Kurian, the Gemini agent operates across web, desktop, mobile, command-line interfaces, and third-party collaboration platforms. Kurian stated that rather than giving the software line-by-line instructions, workers provide high-level business objectives, delegating outcomes while the agent coordinates tasks in the background.

According to Google CEO Sundar Pichai, who addressed the conference, nearly 90% of Fortune 100 businesses currently use Gemini Enterprise products, and Gemini counts more than 1 billion monthly active users globally, as reported by TechCrunch. Google is focusing on corporate deployments first to resolve operational challenges surrounding data security, performance scaling, and governance before expanding equivalent autonomous agents to consumer tiers.

How the Gemini Agent Operates Across Enterprise Systems

The Gemini agent is engineered as a unified, headless service that connects directly into tools employees already use, including Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack, as well as developer environments and enterprise databases. According to SiliconANGLE, the agent executes continuously in the cloud, maintaining a consistent context window and personalization graph even when a user switches between mobile and desktop devices or logs off entirely.

Underneath the prompt window, the agent relies on an architecture organized around four explicit tiers of memory. Session memory retains context for specific ongoing tasks spanning multiple days. Semantic memory serves as a structured knowledge base assembled from reading corporate documents and team communications. Procedural memory stores learned routines and custom workflows, including instructions the agent writes for itself. Finally, episodic memory logs an operational record of past actions. Together, these memory layers allow the agent to onboard itself within a company by learning institutional norms, pricing schedules, and team roles, according to Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian.

To interact with broader corporate ecosystems, the agent interfaces with third-party software including Salesforce, ServiceNow, Jira, Confluence, Git, BigQuery, Databricks, PostgreSQL, and Snowflake. It also supports external tools running on the open Model Context Protocol (MCP). Teams can build reusable modular instruction sets, known as skills, and publish them to an internal company registry; while machine learning tools and PySpark code generation are tailored for data scientists and engineers, operational BigQuery reporting skills allow non-technical business users to generate reports using natural language.

Digital Coworkers With Corporate Email and Directory Accounts

Beyond acting as an individual employee's personal assistant, the Gemini agent can be deployed as an autonomous digital team member. In this configuration, the software receives its own corporate persona, including a dedicated email address on the company's domain (such as @agents.company.com), its own calendar, dedicated Google Drive storage, and a profile listing in the corporate employee directory, as reported by TNW.

Coworker agents can be tagged or directly messaged in Google Chat, or assigned tasks in shared documents. For example, a project manager can assign an event-planning agent to coordinate with regional leads; the agent inspects past chat spaces and calendar availability, drafts correspondence, schedules meeting slots, and compiles briefing presentations across Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides without requiring staff to supply email lists manually. For lengthy workflows, the primary agent can dynamically spin up temporary sub-agents to divide parallel responsibilities before assembling a unified final deliverable.

Multi-Model Architecture and Smart Routing

Google Cloud designed the Gemini agent to separate the execution layer from the underlying neural network. The agent uses an automated mechanism termed Smart Routing to assign individual tasks to the most cost-effective and capable model available. Tasks requiring quick lookups or concise formatting can run on smaller models like Gemini Flash, while extensive reasoning and complex code generation can be dispatched to larger frontier models such as Argon, according to SiliconANGLE.

The system also incorporates third-party models directly into its execution loop. Google announced that Anthropic's Claude model family is fully supported within the agent at launch, with additional proprietary and open-weight models scheduled for future integration. Google Cloud noted that early corporate adopters such as sportswear brand On, Shopify, and PayPal have used multi-model routing to optimize operational costs and turnaround speeds.

To prevent autonomous processes from accumulating unexpected expenses, Google added project-level financial controls inside the Cloud Billing Console. Administrators can enforce real-time spending caps; if an agent's token consumption hits the budgetary ceiling, its background tasks pause automatically until an administrator grants explicit approval to proceed, according to VentureBeat.

Security, Attestation, and Governance Controls

Assigning autonomous agents independent operational roles introduces distinct security and compliance requirements. Google Cloud reported that every coworker agent operates under a cryptographically attested identity stamped into all system logs and virtual machines it provisions. Workflows pass through an enterprise "Agent Gateway" that functions as an AI network firewall, evaluating outbound network requests against internal corporate permissions.

Actions taken across systems are attributed directly to the agent's unique audit log rather than to the credentials of the employee who submitted the initial prompt. This distinction addresses mounting regulatory scrutiny in Europe and the United Kingdom, where the Information Commissioner's Office opened a call for evidence regarding autonomous agent liabilities just hours prior to Google's announcement, as detailed by TNW. However, while Google outlined permission provisioning via OAuth and network isolation containers, it did not detail specific mechanisms for administrative offboarding or revoking an established agent identity.

Enterprise Rollout, Pricing, and Market Context

Google Cloud confirmed that the Gemini agent entered private preview on October 8, 2026, for a selected group of enterprise clients, with broader deployment planned across Google Workspace Business and Enterprise tiers soon. According to VentureBeat, the capability will be included at no additional charge for qualifying Gemini Enterprise accounts, operating as an integrated software update rather than a separate standalone product. Google has not yet clarified whether provisioning distinct Workspace accounts and email addresses for digital coworkers will require incremental user license fees, what limits exist on the number of coworkers an organization can deploy, or whether usage-based charges will apply to sub-agents.

The announcement intensifies competition across the workplace automation sector. Microsoft introduced its expanded Copilot architecture on September 25, combining its background Autopilot agent with multi-model support. OpenAI showcased always-on Dots agents during its DevDay event on September 29, and Anthropic rolled out native Workspace editing integrations on October 6. Meanwhile, SpaceXAI expanded persistent virtual workers with Grok Bot and Team Bots in August and September. Google enters this competitive space backed by broad institutional deployments, citing existing customer implementations including BNP Paribas deploying Gemini to 65,000 staff, Bradesco accelerating document review cycles, and Japanese insurer SOMPO running more than 10,000 custom internal agents, according to Google Cloud.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Google Gemini agent?

The Gemini agent is a universal enterprise AI system that performs autonomous knowledge work, code generation, and multi-step tasks across corporate systems based on high-level goals rather than step-by-step prompts.

Can the Gemini agent use non-Google AI models?

Yes. Google Cloud designed the agent to orchestrate workloads across its own Gemini family as well as Anthropic's Claude models, with support for additional proprietary and open-weight models planned.

How does a digital coworker agent receive its identity?

Coworker agents are provisioned with their own cryptographically attested identity, an internal corporate email address (such as @agents.company.com), dedicated calendar access, Google Drive storage, and an entry in the enterprise employee directory.

When will the Gemini agent be generally available?

The agent launched in private preview on October 8, 2026, for select customers. Broader rollout to eligible Gemini Enterprise and Google Workspace commercial subscriptions is promised soon, though Google has not published an exact general availability date.
