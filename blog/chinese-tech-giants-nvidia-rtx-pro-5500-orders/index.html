Chinese Tech Giants Order $35B in Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 GPUs

ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent have placed massive orders for export-compliant workstation cards, but Nvidia cannot meet the demand.

In short

  • ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent ordered more than 3 million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 workstation GPUs with a combined list price topping $35 billion.
  • Total orders from Chinese buyers have exceeded 6 million units, while Nvidia expects to manufacture no more than 3 million globally in 2027.
  • Nvidia warned partners that fulfillments may be capped at just 5% to 10% of ordered quantities as it prioritizes next-generation data-center chips.
  • Chinese tech companies plan to deploy the export-compliant cards primarily for AI inference workloads rather than model training.

Major Chinese technology companies have placed orders for more than 3 million export-compliant Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 workstation graphics cards, representing a combined sticker value exceeding $35 billion. According to reporting by Reuters via Euronext, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent are behind the rush, seeking hardware that complies with United States export curbs while still providing computing horsepower for artificial intelligence workloads.

However, Tom's Hardware reported that Nvidia has alerted customers and partners that it cannot guarantee fulfillment. Demand from Chinese buyers has far outstripped planned production runs for the workstation component.

Severe Supply Bottlenecks and Allocation Caps

Sources told Reuters (and The Next Web reported the Reuters account without independent verification) that total orders from China have exceeded 6 million units for the card, which carries a list price of roughly $11,400. In response, Nvidia indicated that it can manufacture no more than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 cards globally throughout 2027.

As a consequence of this production ceiling, Nvidia has told supply chain partners they might receive only 5% to 10% of their requested quantities. The supply squeeze stems directly from Nvidia's foundry allocation decisions. Two people familiar with the matter stated that Nvidia expects to commit the bulk of its advanced manufacturing capacity at Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSMC) to Rubin, its upcoming platform for massive data-center clusters, starting in 2027. TSMC declined to comment on the matter.

Early samples of the RTX PRO 5500 are expected to reach customers in December 2026, with commercial shipments slated to commence in the first quarter of 2027, according to Quartz.

Other domestic enterprises are also competing for access. In an October 13, 2026 regulatory filing, Shenzhen-listed financial technology firm Tansun Technology revealed that it contracted to purchase 20,000 RTX PRO 5500 units from an unnamed seller. Tansun specifically cautioned investors that overseas technology controls, broader industry demand, and acute component shortages could cut delivered volumes or delay shipments.

Workstation Hardware Repurposed for Inference

The RTX PRO 5500 was designed primarily for powerful professional workstations and high-end personal computers rather than the scaled compute fabrics that power modern hyperscale facilities. Even so, it has emerged as one of the most capable processors Nvidia can legally deliver to Chinese customers.

Washington's export controls prohibit Nvidia from shipping its Blackwell-based B300 accelerator to mainland China. Meanwhile, shipments of the older Hopper-based H200 require individual case-by-case reviews and strict licensing permits from the U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security. In August 2026, Nvidia stated it anticipated no data-center revenue from its initial H200 shipments to China.

To navigate these limits, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent plan to deploy the workstation cards primarily for inference tasks, sources told Reuters. Inference involves executing trained artificial intelligence models to answer user queries and run chatbots, a phase that generally requires less computing power than training models on massive datasets.

Product specifications reviewed by Reuters indicate that the RTX PRO 5500 offers performance comparable to the RTX 6000D. Nvidia introduced the RTX 6000D in mid-2025 as a scaled-down product configured to meet U.S. performance thresholds. Chinese tech firms initially showed minimal interest in the RTX 6000D, hoping Washington would soften export rules to allow faster processors. That prospect has dissolved, and Nvidia has since ceased production of the RTX 6000D, causing its secondary market price in China to double.

Trade Restrictions and the Domestic Market

The pivot toward workstation hardware illustrates how American restrictions have reshaped hardware procurement in China. Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang previously observed that U.S. export controls eroded Nvidia's share of China's AI data-center market from a near monopoly down to effectively zero.

An Nvidia spokesperson told Reuters that domestic Chinese developers of workstation chips and cards have experienced "unprecedented growth since 2022," the year U.S. export restrictions were first enacted.

"US firms continue to be restricted by a combination of outdated US export controls, which cover gaming products released nearly a half a decade ago, and China's own limit on US imports," the spokesperson said.

Faced with constrained imports, Chinese companies have increasingly turned to domestic chipmakers like Huawei, which has expanded its market share despite headwinds in accessing advanced foreign semiconductor fabrication equipment. Alibaba has also pursued internal designs, introducing its Zhenwu V900 processor in September 2026. Simultaneously, U.S. officials continue to examine how Chinese tech firms access advanced compute power through cloud rental arrangements.

ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security, and China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology all declined or did not respond to requests for comment regarding the order volumes.

Questions people ask

Why are Chinese companies buying workstation cards instead of data-center GPUs?

U.S. export controls ban sales of Nvidia's Blackwell B300 chips to China and require strict case-by-case licensing for the H200. The RTX PRO 5500 is compliant with current export restrictions, making it one of the most powerful Nvidia chips Chinese companies can legally purchase.

How will ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent use the RTX PRO 5500?

The companies plan to use the cards primarily for AI inference, which involves serving user queries and generating responses from trained models. Inference requires less computing power than training large neural networks.

Why can Nvidia fulfill only a small portion of the orders?

Chinese orders for the RTX PRO 5500 have exceeded 6 million units, but Nvidia plans to build no more than 3 million cards globally in 2027. Nvidia is allocating the majority of its foundry capacity at TSMC toward its next-generation Rubin data-center architecture.
