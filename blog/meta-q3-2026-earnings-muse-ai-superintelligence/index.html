Meta Posts $64.6B Q3 Revenue as Zuckerberg Pushes Muse AI

Meta posted a 26 percent increase in quarterly sales to $64.6 billion, while navigating a $9.9 billion legal settlement and ramping spending on its Muse assistant.

In short

  • Meta Platforms reported Q3 2026 revenue of $64.60 billion, up 26% year-over-year, beating Wall Street consensus of roughly $63.3 billion.
  • Reported net income reached $9.86 billion, dampened by a $9.89 billion legal charge over teen mental health settlements; adjusted earnings stood at $7.40 per share.
  • Meta's new personal AI assistant, Muse, surpassed 10 million users within weeks of its September launch, with Mark Zuckerberg reporting that over a quarter of engaged users use it for commerce.
  • Full-year capital expenditures guidance was narrowed to between $135 billion and $145 billion, with Q3 capex reaching $34.91 billion.
  • Meta announced plans to create a new class of non-voting Class C shares scheduled to trade in 2027 to finance infrastructure, talent acquisition, and corporate development.

Meta Platforms delivered $64.60 billion in revenue for the third quarter of 2026, marking a 26 percent year-over-year increase that exceeded Wall Street expectations. Reporting its financial results on October 21, 2026, the social media giant attributed top-line growth to strong digital advertising demand and early momentum in its consumer AI initiatives. Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg stated that Meta hit major milestones this quarter toward delivering personal superintelligence, anchored by the rollout of its Muse assistant and the Muse Wonder frontier model.

While revenue surpassed consensus forecasts of $63.3 billion, profitability reflected heavy legal and capital commitments. Meta posted a net income of $9.86 billion, or $3.83 per share, rising from $2.71 billion a year earlier. Operating profit dropped 37 percent to $12.99 billion as operating margin narrowed from 40 percent to 20 percent, burdened by a $9.89 billion legal charge stemming from an agreement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental health claims. Excluding that settlement, operating income reached $22.88 billion, with adjusted diluted earnings per share standing at $7.40, according to thestreet.com.

Muse Adoption and Agentic Shopping Take Center Stage

The third-quarter earnings call centered heavily on Muse, the conversational assistant Meta introduced in September 2026. Zuckerberg told investors that Muse has already crossed 10 million users, confirming data published by market intelligence tracker Sensor Tower and reported by TechCrunch. Internal figures indicate that approximately 25 percent of people who set up the assistant now use it daily, an engagement rate Zuckerberg described as more than double the starting activity of Threads or competing AI applications.

Beyond basic chat and email management, commerce has quickly emerged as Muse's primary driver. According to Zuckerberg, over 25 percent of engaged users have conducted shopping transactions directly through the assistant, with an average basket size topping $200. Meta integrated fintech firm Plaid's connector to allow Muse to interface directly with customer financial accounts and execute purchases. Zuckerberg cited feedback from Plaid indicating transaction velocity on the connector has outpaced any service the firm has supported to date.

To safeguard user credentials and transactional details, Meta runs the service on what it terms "Muse Secure VM," with an isolated "Muse Confidential VM" architecture currently in development to ensure even Meta cannot inspect user content. Company leadership projected that as Muse scales, its annual revenue per user will surpass the average monetization of Facebook and Instagram, which currently sits in the hundreds of dollars annually.

Ballooning Infrastructure Spend and Capital Restructuring

Powering these generative models requires unprecedented compute resources, a dynamic that drove third-quarter free cash flow to negative $2.12 billion, down from positive cash flow a year prior, as reported by english.news.cn. Meta reported $34.91 billion in third-quarter capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance leases, up 80 percent from $19.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025. Chief Financial Officer Susan Li narrowed Meta's full-year 2026 capex guidance to between $135 billion and $145 billion, raising the lower bound from the earlier estimate of $130 billion.

To fund ongoing data center construction and talent acquisition, Meta announced plans to create a new class of publicly listed, non-voting Class C common stock, according to The Verge. Distributed as a one-time stock dividend, the measure will carry the economic effect of a 2-for-1 stock split when trading commences in January 2027, preserving the company's existing voting structure and maintaining voting power unchanged while expanding capital-raising flexibility.

CFO Susan Li affirmed that aggressive AI spending will continue into 2027. Zuckerberg noted that while Muse remains compute-intensive today, Meta targets a more than tenfold improvement in model cost efficiency over the next 12 months as it prepares infrastructure capable of serving more than one billion users.

Advertising Engine Shows Robust Price Growth

Underpinning Meta's massive AI outlays is a steady core advertising business. Advertising revenue climbed to $63.1 billion during the quarter, up from $39.9 billion in Q3 2024, according to slides analyzed by investing.com. Total Family Daily Active People (DAP) across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger expanded by 3 percent year-over-year to 3.64 billion users in September, adding roughly 100 million individuals.

Monetization efficiency showed sharp gains. The average price per ad jumped 14 percent worldwide year-over-year, including a 23 percent surge in the United States and Canada, even as worldwide ad impression growth moderated to 10 percent. CFO Susan Li stated that Meta's AI investments are producing substantial incremental advertising revenue through stronger engagement, better recommendations, and more efficient monetization.

Reality Labs, the division encompassing augmented reality hardware and virtual reality software, logged third-quarter revenue of $443 million alongside an operating loss of $4.1 billion. Management expects full-year 2026 Reality Labs losses of $18 billion to $19 billion before declining to roughly $15 billion in 2027. Zuckerberg stated that Muse will roll out to Meta smart glasses starting in November 2026, repositioning hardware sales as an onboarding funnel for software and agent subscriptions.

Regulatory Headwinds and Future Guidance

Despite positive market sentiment—which pushed Meta shares up nearly 4 percent in after-hours trading to $723.93—substantial legal uncertainties remain. The $9.89 billion accounting charge resolved a significant portion of teen safety claims across 52 states, but total obligations under that multistate settlement could reach $18.59 billion, according to details disclosed by thestreet.com.

Compounding Meta's legal exposure, State District Judge Francis Mathew recently ordered the company to pay $25 billion in civil penalties after a New Mexico jury found Meta liable for violating state consumer protection laws in connection with historic Cambridge Analytica data harvesting. Meta plans to appeal the verdict. Internally, longtime Chief Global Affairs Officer Joel Kaplan is scheduled to step down in 2027, with President and Vice Chairman Dina Powell McCormick absorbing his regulatory and government affairs duties.

Looking to the final quarter of the year, Meta projected revenue between $73 billion and $76 billion.

What is Meta Muse and how fast is it growing?

Muse is Meta's consumer AI personal assistant launched in September 2026. It reached more than 10 million users within roughly six weeks, with 25 percent daily active retention and over a quarter of engaged users utilizing it for agentic shopping.

Why is Meta issuing a new Class C stock?

Meta plans to issue non-voting Class C common stock in January 2027 via a stock dividend functioning like a 2-for-1 split. The structure provides funding flexibility for AI infrastructure, capital expenditures, acquisitions, and employee compensation without diluting voting control.

How much is Meta spending on capital expenditures in 2026?

Meta narrowed its full-year 2026 capital expenditures outlook to between $135 billion and $145 billion, having spent $34.91 billion on capex and finance lease payments during the third quarter alone.
