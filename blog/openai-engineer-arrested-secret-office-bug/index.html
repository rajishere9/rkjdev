OpenAI Engineer Arrested Over Hidden Bug and Saudi Connections

Federal authorities charged Abdulrahman Alfozan after discovering an encrypted audio recorder hidden in an executive conference room at OpenAI's headquarters.

In short

  • Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old OpenAI engineer and Saudi national, was arrested on October 9, 2026, for allegedly bugging an executive conference room.
  • Federal prosecutors allege the device was built using a $14 microcontroller and researched using chatbot prompts under his wife's account.
  • OpenAI reported that internal AI models assisted forensic teams in tracing the hidden device back to Alfozan, who has been fired.
  • The Justice Department noted that Alfozan had booked one-way tickets to Saudi Arabia for himself and family members prior to his arrest.

Federal authorities arrested OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan on October 9, 2026, charging him with concealing an encrypted audio recording device behind a television in an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco offices. According to a Department of Justice press release, the 35-year-old Saudi national appeared in federal court on October 13, 2026, facing charges of using an electronic device to intercept oral communications. The arrest has brought scrutiny to Alfozan's background in Silicon Valley and his ongoing ties to the Saudi Arabian technology sector.

Discovery of the Device and OpenAI's Investigation

OpenAI personnel discovered the recording device on September 30, 2026, plugged into a USB port on the back of a television inside an executive conference room, according to the DOJ complaint. Prosecutors stated that the device was installed on or about September 28, 2026, wrapped in toilet paper to disguise its presence, and held encrypted files. The conference room hosted high-level discussions covering proprietary research, business development, product roadmaps, and government engagements.

OpenAI stated publicly on X that it fired the employee and immediately referred the matter to law enforcement, as reported by Business Insider. OpenAI confirmed there was no evidence that model weights, source code, or customer data were stolen or improperly accessed during the incident. In reporting by RuntimeWire, OpenAI also stated that it utilized its own AI models alongside forensic investigators to analyze the hardware and determine that an internal employee had planted it.

Investigators linked Alfozan to the scene using building security systems and digital forensics. Closed-circuit television (CCTV) footage captured Alfozan entering the executive room on October 5, 2026, and looking behind the television where the unit was found, according to reporting by BBC News. Later that day, Alfozan left a bag unattended in another area of the OpenAI facility. Security personnel inspected the bag and recovered his wallet, official identification, and a second recording device similar to the one recovered from behind the television.

Hardware Details and Chatbot Search History

Court filings detailed in Tom's Hardware identified the hardware as a Seeed Xiao ESP32-S3 Sense. The component is a thumb-sized development board that retails for $14 on Seeed Studio's website and $24 on Amazon. It includes an onboard microphone, microSD card storage, a detachable camera sensor, and support for 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy, allowing recorded audio to be downloaded over local wireless connections.

Digital evidence gathered by federal investigators showed extensive preparation. Prosecutors stated that Alfozan queried internal productivity systems to gather details about OpenAI executive calendars, room seating capacities, executive office locations, and upcoming meetings with external partners, according to the DOJ complaint.

Additionally, federal investigators documented that a chatbot account linked to Alfozan's spouse submitted technical prompts between September 24 and September 30, 2026. As reported by The Decoder, the inquiries sought guidance on building continuous audio recording systems, adjusting Wi-Fi settings, encrypting SD cards, and improving live listening feeds. The account also submitted queries referencing the specific hardware identifiers and unique MAC address of the exact board found inside OpenAI's conference room.

Background and Gulf Connections

A profile by Business Insider detailed Alfozan's journey from Saudi Arabia to prominent roles in the American tech industry. Raised in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province, he participated in Mawhiba, the national gifted student program, which sponsored his first visit to the United States for an Olympiad training camp in Texas in 2008. He completed his undergraduate studies in computer science and engineering at MIT, completing internships at Microsoft, SAP, and Akamai Technologies.

Following graduation, Alfozan worked as an engineer at Facebook, later becoming a founding engineer at Y Combinator-backed startup Nash and a partner at Saudi venture firm RAED Ventures. While living in California, he remained closely tied to his home country, co-founding Saudi Silicon Valley—a nonprofit consulting organization connecting American tech talent with Saudi enterprises—and hosting a weekly discussion group called "Sunday @ 6" for Gulf tech professionals. He joined OpenAI in July 2025, working on a product team that released its first application in September 2026.

Prior to his arrest, the Justice Department stated in court filings that Alfozan, his wife, and another relative had purchased one-way airline tickets to Saudi Arabia. Defense attorney William Pernik cautioned against assumptions regarding his client's motives, telling Business Insider that the accusations were out of character. Pernik stressed that Alfozan was charged solely under a federal recording statute rather than with stealing trade secrets or acting as a foreign agent.

Legal Implications and Federal Focus

Alfozan faces one federal count of using or endeavoring to use an electronic, mechanical, or other device to intercept oral communications. If convicted, the charge carries a maximum penalty of five years in federal prison, a $250,000 fine, and three years of supervised release, according to the DOJ.

FBI Director Kash Patel stated that Alfozan allegedly sought to capture communications concerning the company's advanced artificial intelligence developments. Tom's Hardware reported that the incident represents the first known arrest of an individual for placing a physical monitoring bug inside a major American artificial intelligence firm. The FBI stated that its counterintelligence investigation remains active to determine whether Alfozan acted alone or on behalf of third parties.
