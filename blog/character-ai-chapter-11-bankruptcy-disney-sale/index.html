Character.AI Enters Chapter 11 with $20M Disney IP Deal

Character Technologies faces a potential wind-down after filing for bankruptcy in Delaware with up to $100 million in liabilities and a $20 million stalking-horse bid from Disney.

In short

  • Character Technologies filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, 2026, reporting between $50 million and $100 million in both assets and liabilities.
  • Disney Worldwide Services agreed to act as a stalking-horse bidder, offering $20 million for a non-exclusive perpetual license to certain intellectual property and transition services.
  • A coalition of attorneys general from 39 states and Washington, D.C., filed a court notice demanding protections for user data and restrictions on chatbot features.
  • Character.AI currently retains approximately 12 million monthly active users, but warns the platform will be wound down if no workable purchase proposal emerges by late November or early December 2026.

Character Technologies Inc., the startup behind consumer chatbot service Character.AI, has filed for voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. Announced on October 12, 2026, the filing initiates a court-supervised sale process aimed at finding a buyer for all or substantially all of the company’s assets. According to an official update on the Character.AI blog, the platform will remain accessible during proceedings, but the company warned that if the sale process fails to produce a viable offer to maintain operations, the service will be wound down.

The bankruptcy comes alongside a proposed stalking-horse transaction. Prior to entering bankruptcy, Character Technologies entered into an agreement with Disney Worldwide Services, Inc., which agreed to pay $20 million for a non-exclusive perpetual license to certain intellectual property and claims, as outlined in a company press release distributed by Business Wire. Under Section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, the agreement establishes a floor price and remains open to higher or better bids through a court-approved auction expected to wrap up by late November or early December 2026.

Assets, Liabilities, and Disney Transition Services

Court filings show that Character Technologies estimated both its assets and liabilities between $50 million and $100 million, as reported by Mashable. The company’s board of directors formed a restructuring committee on October 8, 2026, to evaluate cash flow constraints, liquidity, and litigation liabilities before approving the bankruptcy petition four days later. Despite mounting losses, the debtor entered Chapter 11 with no funded debt and roughly $57 million in unrestricted cash, according to a case summary published by Bondoro.

The transaction with Disney also involves staffing resources. Former Character.AI employees who recently joined Disney—including former CEO Karandeep Singh Anand—will provide transition services to help the startup safely operate its infrastructure during the court proceedings, according to Business Wire. Character Technologies entered bankruptcy with its workforce reduced to 18 active full-time staff members, down sharply after Disney hired away a substantial portion of its team. The company relies on independent contractors and temporary agency personnel for engineering, moderation, data science, and operational roles.

The relationship between the entertainment giant and the AI platform has not always been collaborative. In September 2025, Disney sent Character.AI a cease-and-desist letter regarding user-made chatbots that impersonated characters from Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar, which prompted Character.AI to remove the infringing personas, according to Bondoro. Crucially, the $20 million stalking-horse deal does not include user data or chat logs, meaning any transfer of conversational data would have to be handled under separate bids.

State Attorneys General Demand Privacy and Child Safety Protections

The prospect of user data being sold off under bankruptcy proceedings has triggered swift regulatory pushback. On October 14, 2026, Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser led a coalition of attorneys general from 39 states and the District of Columbia in submitting a formal notice to the Delaware bankruptcy court, as announced by the Colorado Attorney General's Office. The coalition expressed concern over the millions of private, sensitive logs gathered by the company—including user discussions regarding mental health, therapy, and personal relationships.

Regulators highlighted the platform's history of user safety controversies. Multiple states began inquiries after a 2024 wrongful death lawsuit alleged that the service's conversational bots fostered an emotionally harmful bond with a 14-year-old boy in Florida who died by suicide. In a sworn court declaration, Character.AI Chief Executive Officer Colin Adams acknowledged that 46 states and territories are currently investigating the firm, as reported by KQED.

To safeguard consumers, the state coalition outlined nine specific conditions that any winning bidder should meet, according to KQED. These conditions include instituting mandatory age verification, banning AI chatbots that mimic professionals like doctors, therapists, or lawyers, prohibiting the use of historical user logs to train future models, and giving every user the opportunity to erase their data prior to a sale closing. The attorneys general also reserved the option to ask the court to appoint an independent consumer privacy ombudsman to examine any asset transfers.

High Compute Costs and the Road to Chapter 11

Character Technologies was founded in late 2021 by former Google researchers Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, achieving a $1 billion valuation in March 2023 following a $150 million Series A funding round, according to Bondoro. In August 2024, the startup entered an unusual reverse acquihire arrangement with Google: Google paid approximately $2.7 billion in cash for a non-exclusive model license while rehiring Shazeer and De Freitas. Proceeds from the deal funded a buyout of venture capitalists at a $2.5 billion valuation, converted equity to current and former employees, and saw roughly 20 percent of staff depart for Google DeepMind.

Following that deal, Character.AI abandoned the practice of pretraining foundation models from scratch due to high computational overhead, shifting instead toward fine-tuning open-weight models such as Meta's Llama, Alibaba's Qwen, and DeepSeek, as documented by Bondoro. Nonetheless, running inference for interactive user engagement proved difficult to balance against top-line receipts. In the second half of 2024, monthly operating expenses averaged $21.9 million—including $13.8 million in compute expenses alone—against a net monthly cash burn of $21.5 million.

Cost-cutting measures gradually reduced operating expenses to approximately $13.3 million per month by August 2026, lowering net monthly cash burn to $8.2 million, according to Bondoro. Revenue expanded from $7.4 million in fiscal year 2024 to $23.6 million in fiscal year 2025, reaching $5.3 million in August 2026 across subscriptions, advertising, and in-app purchases. However, the gains were outpaced by compute costs, declining user engagement—monthly active users dropped from 20 million in late 2024 to roughly 12 million in 2026—and heavy legal expenses arising from state investigations and personal injury litigation. Whether the service continues will now depend on whether an outside buyer steps forward to top Disney's initial bid before December.

Questions people ask

Is Character.AI shutting down immediately?

No. Character Technologies confirmed that the platform remains active and that paid subscriptions continue as normal during the Chapter 11 process. However, if the court-supervised auction fails to find a buyer willing to continue operations, the platform will be wound down.

What did Disney agree to purchase?

Disney Worldwide Services entered into a $20 million stalking-horse agreement for a non-exclusive perpetual license to certain intellectual property and claims. Disney also agreed to provide transition services using former Character.AI employees it recently hired.

Is Disney acquiring Character.AI user chats or personal data?

No. The $20 million licensing agreement with Disney does not include user data or conversation history. Any sale or transfer of user chats would require a separate transaction, which faces scrutiny from a coalition of state attorneys general.

What restrictions are state attorneys general seeking in the bankruptcy?

A coalition of attorneys general across 39 states and D.C. submitted demands including mandatory age verification, the elimination of personas impersonating therapists or medical professionals, limits on training AI with past conversations, and the right for users to delete their data prior to any sale.
