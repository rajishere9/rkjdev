Nvidia Weighs Billions in Credit Guarantees for Nscale and Anthropic

The proposed arrangement would help finance a 460-megawatt facility in West Virginia running on Vera Rubin chips under a computing pact worth roughly $45 billion.

In short

  • Nvidia is negotiating billions of dollars in credit guarantees to back Nscale's debt for a 460-megawatt data center buildout in West Virginia.
  • Anthropic plans to utilize the computing capacity in an arrangement tied to commitments valued at roughly $45 billion.
  • The facility is planned around Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips at Nscale's Monarch campus, occupying space previously eyed by Microsoft.
  • If finalized, the structure puts Nvidia in three roles simultaneously: hardware supplier, equity investor, and debt backstop.

Nvidia is in discussions to provide billions of dollars in credit guarantees to support debt raised by British AI cloud provider Nscale, backing a massive data center buildout in West Virginia intended for AI lab Anthropic, according to a report by The Information. The debt financing would support the construction and power infrastructure for a 460-megawatt facility tied to an Anthropic computing agreement valued at approximately $45 billion. Discussions remain ongoing, no agreement has been signed, and terms may change.

The talks signal a deepening financial alignment between chipmakers, specialized infrastructure operators, and foundation model creators. By offering credit backstops, Nvidia is moving beyond its traditional role as a component vendor to assist infrastructure providers in securing the massive capital required to purchase its high-performance silicon.

Inside the 460-Megawatt West Virginia Project

The infrastructure under negotiation centers on a second building at Nscale's Monarch campus located in West Virginia, according to reporting summarized by investingLive. The site is slated to deploy Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips.

Anthropic stepped in to take over the West Virginia site after Microsoft walked away from a preliminary letter of intent signed in March 2026, investingLive reported. In August 2026, Anthropic agreed to spend $45 billion leasing computing capacity at Nscale's campus, and separate discussions covering an additional 460 megawatts of capacity carry an estimated value of another $45 billion, according to UA.NEWS. Filings tied to Nscale's planned public listing confirm existing infrastructure commitments from Anthropic approaching $45 billion.

Nvidia, Anthropic, and Nscale did not respond to requests for comment regarding the discussions, Reuters reported via UA.NEWS.

Why Nvidia Credit Guarantees Matter for Neoclouds

If completed, the arrangement would make Nvidia a chip supplier, an equity shareholder, and a debt guarantor for Nscale. Nscale publicly filed for a United States initial public offering in September 2026 targeting a valuation of roughly $30 billion, after securing a $3.4 billion pre-IPO funding round led by Third Point with Nvidia participating as an investor, investingLive noted.

Nscale belongs to a rising category of specialized cloud providers, often called neoclouds, that focus specifically on high-density AI clusters. As The Information reported, Nvidia has signaled financial backing across several such firms, including CoreWeave and Firmus. In September 2026, Anthropic also concluded a separate $35 billion computing capacity agreement with Lambda, another cloud operator backed by Nvidia.

Credit guarantees from a highly rated, cash-rich supplier like Nvidia can significantly lower borrowing costs for capital-intensive infrastructure builders. Data center developers must borrow billions upfront to finance construction and energy infrastructure long before server clusters generate revenue. Backing from their primary hardware provider helps compress credit spreads and accelerate project delivery schedules.

Scrutiny Over AI Debt and Circular Financing

The prospect of vendor-backed debt guarantees has renewed debate over circular financing structures across the AI supply chain. Critics argue that when a hardware supplier holds equity in a cloud company, guarantees that company's debt, and then books revenue from the chips that debt finances, the supplier is essentially underwriting demand for its own products. Nvidia's finance chief has said the company sees those arrangements differently.

Market analysts are monitoring the balance-sheet implications of such transactions. According to investingLive, Morgan Stanley recently identified surging AI-related debt issuance and widening credit spreads on data center bonds as notable sources of market pressure. For Nvidia, entering contingent credit commitments creates off-balance-sheet exposure tied to the execution capabilities of pre-IPO operators. For Nscale, prospective IPO investors will evaluate the durability of revenue streams that rely heavily on financial support provided by the firm's main silicon supplier.
