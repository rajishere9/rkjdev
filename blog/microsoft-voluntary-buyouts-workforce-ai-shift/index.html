Microsoft Offers Voluntary Buyouts to Up to 60,000 Employees

The company opened a voluntary exit program to roughly 27% of its workforce across the US, UK, Canada, and India to manage surging AI infrastructure costs.

In short

  • Microsoft introduced a Voluntary Exit Offer open to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its 223,000-person global workforce.
  • Eligible staff include workers at level 70 (vice president) and below across participating teams in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and India.
  • The buyout offer comes as Microsoft projected it will spend $175 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, while capital expenditures were projected to exceed $50 billion for the September quarter.
  • Critical units including data centers, security, and parts of commercial business and marketing are excluded from the voluntary departure program.

On October 26, 2026, Microsoft announced that it is offering voluntary exit packages to approximately 60,000 employees across the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, and India. The program, known internally as the Voluntary Exit Offer (VEO), makes Microsoft voluntary buyouts available to roughly 27% of the company's global workforce of 223,000 people. Executive Vice President and Chief People Officer Amy Coleman disclosed the decision in a memo published on The Official Microsoft Blog, describing it as a way for participating organizations to adapt to changing operational demands.

The large-scale buyout push arrives as Microsoft balances unprecedented capital outlays for artificial intelligence infrastructure against corporate margins. Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood stated in July 2026 that the company expects to spend $175 billion on AI infrastructure during calendar year 2026 alone, as reported by CBS News. By offering exit incentives rather than immediate involuntary cuts across the board, Microsoft seeks to trim staffing expenses while reallocating capital to data centers and compute capacity.

Terms and Eligibility for the Microsoft Voluntary Buyouts

The Voluntary Exit Offer targets staff at Microsoft experience level 70 and below, which reaches up to the vice president level, according to Bloomberg. Eligible personnel received an email containing individual offer details on October 26, 2026. Workers who opt into the departure package must make a decision by early December 2026, with an expected exit date of December 31, 2026, according to reporting from CNBC.

An internal human resources advisory viewed by The Verge noted that eligibility is not tied to age, length of service, or individual performance ratings. That marks a direct shift from Microsoft's Voluntary Retirement Program offered earlier in 2026, which applied only to approximately 9,000 US employees whose age plus years of service totaled 70 or higher. Microsoft reported in July that over 30% of eligible staff accepted that earlier retirement program.

Participation in the current buyout offer is determined strictly by business organization. Teams participating either fully or in part include AI experiences, Copilot, CoreAI, Windows + Devices, Xbox, finance, human resources, and corporate, external, and legal affairs. Employees in data center operations, cybersecurity, quantum computing, commercial sales, and marketing are not eligible to take the buyout.

There are minor contradictions in external reports regarding subsidiaries. While internal sources cited by The Verge indicated that Xbox team members are among those eligible for VEO packages, a Microsoft spokesperson cited by the Los Angeles Times stated that subsidiaries including LinkedIn, GitHub, Activision Blizzard, and Xbox gaming studios were excluded from the corporate buyout program.

Shifting Capital from Headcount to AI Infrastructure

The buyout program follows a sequence of workforce reductions across Microsoft during 2026. In July 2026, the company cut 4,800 jobs—roughly 2% of its workforce—principally within its gaming and commercial sales divisions, as reported by The Verge. Regulatory filings show total headcount fell from 228,000 at the end of 2025 to 223,000 by late June 2026.

Despite beating analyst expectations in July with fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $90 billion and net income of $35.8 billion, Microsoft's massive compute investments have placed pressure on its balance sheet. Capital expenditures for the quarter ending September 2026 were projected to exceed $50 billion, compared to $34.9 billion in the same quarter a year earlier, as reported by CNBC. Although Microsoft generated $19.64 billion in positive free cash flow in the second quarter of 2026, that figure reflected a 23% decline year over year.

The company has directed vast cloud resources toward foundation model partners, supplying computing infrastructure to OpenAI and Anthropic while driving adoption for Microsoft 365 Copilot, which now counts over 30 million paid seats, according to the Los Angeles Times.

Tech Industry Workforce Adjustments

Microsoft is not alone in thinning staffing levels while funding server capacity and specialized silicon. As detailed by The Guardian, multiple major tech firms have cited artificial intelligence investments when executing workforce reductions in 2026. Meta outlined plans in April to reduce personnel by 10%, cutting roughly 8,000 roles, while Oracle eliminated 21,000 jobs, or nearly 13% of its staff, in June. Amazon also executed successive rounds of layoffs, targeting its retail units.

Yet the link between workforce cuts and measurable productivity gains remains scrutinized. The Guardian highlighted a recent National Bureau of Economic Research working paper surveying 9,000 senior executives worldwide, in which 90% reported that internal AI adoption had not yet generated measurable enterprise productivity improvements. Even within Microsoft, internal friction over output quality has surfaced publicly, with senior executive Ryan Roslansky openly lamenting the rise of low-quality automated content in workplace communication.

Microsoft is scheduled to report third-quarter results on October 28, 2026. Company officials have not stated whether involuntary layoffs will follow if enough employees do not accept buyouts.

Questions people ask

Who is eligible for the Microsoft Voluntary Exit Offer?

The offer is available to employees at level 70 (vice president) and below within participating business units across the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, and India. It excludes staff in data centers, cybersecurity, marketing, and commercial business.

What is the deadline for employees to accept the buyout?

Eligible employees must submit their decisions in early December 2026, with the departure date set for December 31, 2026.

Why is Microsoft offering buyouts instead of regular operations?

Microsoft is seeking capital efficiency and headcount flexibility as it allocates massive sums to artificial intelligence compute, including an anticipated $175 billion in AI infrastructure capital expenditures across 2026.
