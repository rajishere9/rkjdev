AMD Agrees to Acquire World Labs for $8.2 Billion

The all-stock acquisition brings AI pioneer Dr. Fei-Fei Li to AMD as chief scientist to integrate spatial intelligence research directly with hardware development.

In short

  • AMD has signed a definitive agreement to acquire spatial intelligence startup World Labs for approximately $8.2 billion in all-stock.
  • World Labs co-founder Dr. Fei-Fei Li will join AMD as executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting directly to chair and CEO Dr. Lisa Su.
  • The acquisition aims to combine frontier spatial-intelligence model research with semiconductor hardware and systems engineering.
  • The transaction is scheduled to close by the end of 2026, pending regulatory approvals and standard closing conditions.

Semiconductor maker AMD announced a definitive agreement on September 28, 2026, to acquire spatial intelligence startup World Labs in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $8.2 billion. The deal brings one of the prominent research teams developing 3D world models and robotic simulation technology directly under AMD’s corporate umbrella. As part of the agreement, World Labs co-founder Dr. Fei-Fei Li will join AMD as executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting directly to AMD chair and CEO Dr. Lisa Su.

According to AMD's official announcement, the acquisition aims to strengthen the company’s ability to design hardware, software, and systems tailored to emerging frontier AI workloads, including reasoning, robotics, simulation, and physical AI.

Terms of the Acquisition and Leadership Integration

The transaction is structured as an all-stock deal valued at roughly $8.2 billion and is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals, as detailed in AMD's press release.

Upon closing, the World Labs team will continue focusing on AI model research within AMD. World Labs leaders Justin Johnson and Ben Mildenhall will work alongside Li to guide the group, according to an update on the World Labs blog. Li, a Stanford University computer science professor recognized for her pioneering work creating the ImageNet benchmark, founded World Labs in 2024 to build foundation models grounded in the physical world.

“Building the compute platforms for the next generation of AI requires a deep understanding of how models are evolving,” said Dr. Lisa Su in AMD's announcement. “Fei-Fei and the World Labs team bring exceptional research leadership and model expertise. Together, we can use that insight to develop the hardware, software and systems that will power the next generation of AI and strengthen the open AI ecosystem.”

Li stated in the announcement that advancing frontier AI requires tighter collaboration across compute architecture and model design: “Joining AMD will give our team the resources and engineering depth to accelerate our research and help define the infrastructure needed for the next era of AI.”

Deepening Hardware and Model Collaboration

The buyout builds on an existing relationship between the two companies. As reported by TechCrunch, AMD and World Labs established a technical partnership last year focusing on model training and inference optimization on AMD GPUs, and Li appeared as a guest during AMD’s CES presentation earlier in 2026.

World Labs launched in 2024 and achieved a $1 billion valuation within months of its founding, according to The Verge. In 2025, the startup released its first commercial product, Marble, a world-generation model capable of generating, reconstructing, and simulating interactive 3D environments from text, image, and video inputs.

Beyond digital entertainment experiences, spatial models such as Marble are designed to generate synthetic environments for robotic simulation and physical AI learning. Training physical robots—ranging from industrial arms to autonomous systems and humanoids—often suffers from a scarcity of real-world data, making synthetic simulation environments critical for robotic development.

Strategic Context in AI Hardware and Ecosystems

The acquisition represents a major step in AMD’s effort to expand its full-stack software and model ecosystem. While AMD has historically released text and video-oriented models, competitor Nvidia has already fielded open-weight world models such as Cosmos, as noted by TechCrunch.

The deal also arrives amid significant consolidation across AI compute and infrastructure. Earlier in September 2026, Nvidia announced an agreement to acquire AI platform Hugging Face for nearly $13 billion, according to The Verge.

By integrating World Labs' internal research pipelines, AMD aims to directly incorporate the compute demands of next-generation spatial models into future GPU and systems architecture roadmaps while continuing to support open ecosystem deployments.
