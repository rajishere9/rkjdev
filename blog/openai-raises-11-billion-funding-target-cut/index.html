OpenAI Raises $11 Billion as Round Target Drops to $20 Billion

OpenAI has secured $11 billion in preliminary commitments while trimming its fundraising target to $20 billion amid cooling market appetite.

In short

  • OpenAI secured approximately $11 billion in commitments, lowering its ultimate round target from $30 billion to $20 billion.
  • The round values OpenAI at $1.25 trillion post-money, down from the company's initial pitch of $1.4 trillion.
  • A United Arab Emirates consortium, including state-backed fund MGX, is poised to be the largest participant alongside Silicon Valley venture firms.
  • The fundraising recalibration comes amid an AI equity selloff and competition from Anthropic ahead of prospective public listings.

OpenAI has secured approximately $11 billion in commitments for its current financing round, according to reports from The Wall Street Journal cited by TNW. The company originally sought $30 billion but has now reduced its target to $20 billion as investor demand showed signs of slowing. The ongoing transaction values the company at $1.25 trillion post-money, down from the $1.4 trillion valuation OpenAI pitched when launching the round.

The reduction in target highlights shifting investor sentiment across artificial intelligence markets following months of aggressive capital deployment.

Slower Momentum and Reduced Targets

OpenAI originally approached investors last month aiming for a $30 billion round at a $1.4 trillion valuation. As reported by The Financial Times via PYMNTS, the company adjusted its expectations downward after institutional interest proved tepid. OpenAI established a Friday deadline for commitments, which resulted in several institutional investors paring back their planned ticket sizes.

With $11 billion currently locked in, the initial close is expected to fall well short of the revised $20 billion mark. To bridge the remaining gap, OpenAI informed shareholders that it intends to broaden its fundraising efforts to family offices and high-net-worth individuals over the coming weeks, TNW reported. A subsequent retail tranche is planned at the identical $1.25 trillion valuation.

The current round follows a massive $122 billion financing closed in March 2026 at an $852 billion post-money valuation, according to data from Dealroom.co. The downsized appetite just seven months later demonstrates that even the best-capitalized artificial intelligence firms face limits from private capital allocators.

Participating Investors and Compute Access

A United Arab Emirates investment consortium featuring state-backed fund MGX is expected to emerge as the largest backer in the round, according to The Financial Times and Dealroom.co. Beyond capital, the agreement includes an option for OpenAI to lease newly constructed computing capacity within the Gulf region.

Several prominent venture capital firms are also participating in the tranche. The Financial Times reported that SoftBank, Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital, and Andreessen Horowitz plan to deploy capital into the round, though TNW stated it had not independently verified the reports. Despite the participation of these high-profile venture funds, the aggregate capital committed remains less than half of what the company initially sought.

Market Headwinds and Tech Selloffs

The funding round encountered turbulent market conditions. Public equities tied to artificial intelligence have experienced sharp pullbacks, triggering broader caution among institutional financiers. According to Dealroom.co, the tech-heavy Nasdaq fell 2.9% on the final Friday of October and stood 7.8% lower than its record high set on October 6.

Broader tech earnings heightened market unease. Alphabet shares dropped 12% to 13% on October 29 after reporting quarterly earnings, erasing roughly $500 billion in market capitalization, as detailed by PYMNTS. Disappointing results from Amazon and Intel similarly fanned fears regarding the financial sustainability and payoff timeline of massive AI infrastructure spending, Investing.com noted.

Competition with Anthropic and IPO Timelines

OpenAI faces escalating commercial and financial pressure from chief rival Anthropic. As reported by Investing.com, Anthropic's Claude Code product has gained strong adoption among enterprise clients and software engineers. Anthropic more than doubled its revenue between the first and second quarters of 2026 to reach $11.6 billion, generating a modest operating profit during that window.

In contrast, OpenAI posted revenue of $6.7 billion over the same period—representing an 18% sequential increase—while experiencing widening operating losses. OpenAI managed to recapture some market share after lowering prices on its latest artificial intelligence models.

The timing of the round also overlaps with Anthropic's race to public markets. Anthropic publicly filed paperwork on October 26 for an initial public offering slated for November 2026, targeting a valuation of at least $1.5 trillion, TNW reported. Anthropic's IPO registration revealed $16.3 billion in six-month revenue against an $80.7 billion net loss, up 1,300% year over year, according to PYMNTS.

OpenAI has framed its current funding push as an opportunistic bridge round ahead of its own proposed public listing, which could take place as early as next year, according to The Wall Street Journal. As Anthropic prepares to tap public markets first, OpenAI must finalize its remaining private allocations.
